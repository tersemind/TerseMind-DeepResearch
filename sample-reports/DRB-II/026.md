# Comprehensive Report on Inorganic Corrosion Inhibitors (Up to Early 2023): Classification, Reaction Mechanisms, and Industrial Applications

## Executive Overview: Electrochemical Frameworks, Taxonomic Boundaries, and Concentration Thresholds

**Evidence cutoff: 31 March 2023.** Inorganic corrosion inhibitors—crystalline salts, metal oxyanions, colloidal silicates and phosphates, ceramic/spinel metal oxides, and sacrificial metals devoid of $\text{C–H}$ backbones—protect metals at bulk concentrations of $1\text{–}15,000\text{ ppm}$ ($0.0001\text{–}1.5\text{ wt\%}$) or as solid conversion layers and coating pigments (Palanisamy, 2019; Dariva & Galio, 2014; Veolia Water Technologies Handbook [Veolia], Ch. 24). Unlike organic inhibitors (imidazolines, amines, quaternary ammonium salts, phosphonates like $\text{HEDP}$, and phosphate esters that adsorb via $\text{N, S, O, P}$ heteroatoms into 2D hydrophobic monolayers; Brycki et al., 2018; Solovyeva et al., 2023; Gogoi & Barhai, 2011), **inorganic systems construct a 3D solid phase** at the metal–electrolyte boundary, alter interfacial water chemistry (e.g., alkalization), or polarize the substrate galvanically. As noted in a 2023 downhole review: *"Inorganic inhibitors are mainly used in boilers and cooling towers whereas organic inhibitors are mainly used in oil field systems"* (Solovyeva et al., 2023).

In aqueous steel corrosion, iron dissolves anodically ($\text{Fe} \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^-$) while dissolved oxygen/water reduce cathodically ($\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 4e^- \rightarrow 4\text{OH}^-\text{(aq)}$), forming $\text{Fe(OH)}_2\text{(s)}$ that oxidizes into rust (Wikipedia Galvanic anode; Veolia, Ch. 24). The four composition-based categories examined here—**metal-based, metal oxide-based, phosphate-based, and silicate-based inhibitors**—are **neither mutually exclusive nor exhaustive** (e.g., $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ is both metal- and phosphate-based; $\text{Ce}^{3+}$ salts form oxide films; and nitrites, nitrates, borates, and oxygen scavengers are critical additional inorganic families). They operate within a functional electrochemical taxonomy (Palanisamy, 2019; Rajesh KS, 2021; Veolia, Ch. 24; FHWA, 1998):

* **Passivating (Anodic) Inhibitors:** Block anodic oxidation, promote/repair thin passive oxide films, and shift open-circuit corrosion potential ($E_{\text{corr}}$) nobly (Veolia, Ch. 24; Dariva & Galio, 2014). They split into:
  1. *Oxidizing anions* ($\text{CrO}_4^{2-}, \text{NO}_2^-, \text{NO}_3^-$), which reduce directly to passivate steel *without dissolved $\text{O}_2$* (Palanisamy, 2019; Rajesh KS, 2021).
  2. *Non-oxidizing ions* ($\text{PO}_4^{3-}, \text{WO}_4^{2-}, \text{MoO}_4^{2-}$), which **require dissolved $\text{O}_2$** to induce passivity (Palanisamy, 2019; Veolia, Ch. 24).
  Anodic passivators are classed as **"dangerous inhibitors"** if depleted below a critical threshold of $10^{-4}\text{ to }10^{-2}\text{ mol/L}$ (Palanisamy, 2019; Kumar, 2020; AquaChem; FHWA, 1998): incomplete coverage leaves small active anodes coupled to a large passivated cathode, accelerating localized pitting faster than uninhibited uniform attack (Song et al., 2019; Rajesh KS, 2021).
* **Precipitating (Cathodic) Inhibitors, Poisons, and Scavengers:** Slow cathodic reduction and shift $E_{\text{corr}}$ negatively (Rivetti et al., 2018). Classed as **"safe inhibitors"** because barrier coverage scales proportionally with bulk dosage without direct anodic reaction, though requiring higher concentrations (Palanisamy, 2019; Dariva & Galio, 2014). Sub-mechanisms include:
  1. *Cathodic precipitators* ($\text{Zn}^{2+}, \text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Ce}^{3+}, \text{La}^{3+}$, $\text{ZnSO}_4$, $\text{Ca/Mg}$ carbonates, polyphosphates), which precipitate insoluble hydroxides, oxides, carbonates, or phosphates where cathodic $\text{O}_2$ reduction raises local boundary-layer pH by $1\text{–}2$ units above bulk water (Veolia, Ch. 24; Yesu Thangam et al., 2009; Hinton & Arnott, 1986).
  2. *Cathodic poisons* (arsenates, $\text{As}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Sb}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Bi}_2\text{O}_3$, and $\text{Pb}^{2+}, \text{Tl}^+, \text{Mn}^{2+}, \text{Cd}^{2+}$), which deposit in acid media to lower cathodic depolarization overvoltage and impede $\text{H}\cdot + \text{H}\cdot \rightarrow \text{H}_2$ recombination—carrying a major safety hazard by promoting atomic $\text{H}$ absorption, **hydrogen blistering, and hydrogen embrittlement** (Palanisamy, 2019; Rajesh KS, 2021).
  3. *Gas scavengers* ($\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_3, \text{NH}_4\text{HSO}_3, \text{SO}_2, \text{N}_2\text{H}_4$, and $\text{Zn}$ or $\text{NO}_2^-$ $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ scavengers) that chemically strip corrosive gases (Solovyeva et al., 2023; Henke & Carpenter, 2004).
* **Mixed-Mechanism and Galvanic Systems:** Phosphates, silicates, chromate–phosphates, orthophosphate–zinc, polyphosphate–silicate, and zinc–tannin blends exhibit mixed anodic/cathodic action with significant precipitation (Palanisamy, 2019; Chen & Yang, 2019; Rajesh KS, 2021). Separately, **galvanic (sacrificial) metals** ($\text{Mg, Al, Zn}$) electrically coupled to steel corrode preferentially, forcing steel to be the cathode (Pathak et al., 2012; UFC 3-570-01, 2016/2019).

Three framing caveats govern these categories:
1. **Additives vs. Electrodes, Conversion Coatings, and Paints:** Strict aqueous inhibitors are low-dosage fluid additives, whereas sacrificial anodes are consumed electrodes, oxide paints are barrier/active coatings, and phosphating is a thick conversion layer formed by reacting metal with $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$ (Tamilselvi et al., 2015; Kiesow, 2016; US EPA, 2016). In organic coatings, metallic $\text{Zn}$ protects *"by acting as a sacrificial pigment and not as an inhibitive pigment"* (U.S. Bureau of Reclamation [USBR], 2017). Both aqueous and solid/coating systems are explicitly distinguished below.
2. **Pigment Solubility Window and Cross-Category Overlap:** Zinc appears in three distinct roles: elemental $\text{Zn}$ metal, oxidized $\text{ZnO}$ (which cannot supply sacrificial electrons), and $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$. By Sinko's rule for sparingly soluble pigments $\text{A}_m^{n+}\text{B}_n^{m-}$, **spontaneous passivation occurs only when saturation molar solubility exceeds the critical concentration of the inhibitive anion $\text{B}^{m-}$**, while $\text{A}^{n+}$ has minimal influence except for $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ or $\text{Ce}^{3+}$ (USBR, 2017). Excessive solubility ($>2\text{ g/100 mL}$) creates osmotic blistering/delamination under paint films; insufficient solubility causes a **passivation lag time** where bare steel corrodes before enough ions leach into defects (Yin, 2009; USBR, 2017; Gharbi et al., 2018).
3. **Regulatory Shift Away from Chromates and Lead:** Hexavalent chromium ($\text{Cr(VI)}$) was the 90-year benchmark inhibitor (anodic/cathodic across $\text{pH } 2\text{–}12$, $\text{O}_2$-independent, self-healing scratches on AA2024-T3 as mobile $\text{CrO}_4^{2-}$ reduces cathodically to adherent $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3$; Veolia, Ch. 24; USBR, 2017; Gharbi et al., 2018; Zhao et al., 2001). However, $\text{Cr(VI)}$ carcinogenicity (WHO 1972/1980; HHS 1980; NIOSH Pub. 2013-128, Jan 2013) drove the EU RoHS $0.1\%$ limit and **REACH** (2006) Jan 2019 sunset date (individual ECHA authorizations costing $\sim €1.5\text{ M}$; Gharbi et al., 2018). Paired with red lead ($\text{Pb}_3\text{O}_4$) bans, EU marine-toxicity labeling on $\text{ZnO}$ and $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ (Directive 1999/45/EC, ATP-29, 2005; WFD zinc standards, Comber et al., 2008; Gichuhi, 2021), nitrite and $\text{V}_2\text{O}_5$ toxicity limits (Gharbi et al., 2018), and wastewater phosphorus/eutrophication caps (Hazen & Sawyer, 2022; Wan et al., 2022), practice up to early 2023 shifted toward **synergistic multi-ion blends, rare-earth salts, and smart ion-exchange nanocarriers** (Chen & Yang, 2019; Mendez et al., 2022; Tedim et al., 2022; Visser et al., 2019).

# Part One: Main Types and Mechanisms of Action

## 1. Metal-Based Inhibitors

"Metal-based inhibitors" encompass two chemical scopes:
1. **Elemental sacrificial metals/coatings** ($\text{Zn, Al, Mg}$ powders, anodes, galvanized layers; Pathak et al., 2012; USBR, 2017; UFC 3-570-01, 2016/2019) and **soluble multivalent/alkali metal cations** ($\text{Zn}^{2+}, \text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Ni}^{2+}, \text{Ce}^{3+}, \text{La}^{3+}, \text{Li}^+$) acting via cathodic precipitation, $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ scavenging, or autonomous layered double hydroxide ($\text{LDH}$) growth (Veolia, Ch. 24; Onaizi et al., 2020; Visser et al., 2019; Mendez et al., 2022).
2. **Transition-metal oxyanion salts, heteropolyacids, and alkali/alkaline-earth nitrites, nitrates, borates, and pseudohalides** ($\text{CrO}_4^{2-}, \text{MoO}_4^{2-}, \text{WO}_4^{2-}, \text{VO}_3^-, \text{PMo}_{12}\text{O}_{40}^{3-}, \text{NO}_2^-, \text{NO}_3^-, \text{B}_4\text{O}_7^{2-}, \text{SCN}^-$), acting via anodic passivation or interfacial complexation (Palanisamy, 2019; Song et al., 2019; Kumar, 2020; Kazansky et al., 2013). *(Note: Soluble oxoanions like $\text{Na}_2\text{MoO}_4$ and $\text{Na}_2\text{CrO}_4$ are distinct from dispersed solid metal oxides.)*

### 1.1 Basic Working Principle

* **Sacrificial Galvanic Polarization, Self-Passivation vs. Anode Activity, and Sizing:** Elemental $\text{Mg}$ ($E^\circ = -2.372\text{ V}$ vs. SHE at $298.15\text{ K}$), $\text{Al}$ ($-1.662\text{ V}$), and $\text{Zn}$ ($-0.763\text{ V}$) are active relative to $\text{Fe}$ ($-0.447\text{ V}$) (Pathak et al., 2012; Maniam & Paul, 2021). Electrically coupled to steel in a continuous ionic electrolyte, the active metal dissolves ($\text{M} \rightarrow \text{M}^{n+} + ne^-$), supplying electrons for cathodic $\text{O}_2$ reduction ($\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 4e^- \rightarrow 4\text{OH}^-$) or water reduction ($2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{H}_2\uparrow + 2\text{OH}^-$) on steel, suppressing iron dissolution (US Army TM 5-811-7, 1985; UFC 3-570-01, 2016/2019). In $3.5\text{ wt\% NaCl}$, $\text{Mg}$'s open-circuit potential (OCP) is $-1.3\text{ to }-1.5\text{ V}$ vs. $\text{Ag/AgCl}$ against $-0.5\text{ V}$ for AA2024-T3, and a $\text{Mg}$-rich primer polarizes the alloy to $\approx -0.9\text{ V}$ ($\text{OCP} \le -0.9\text{ V}$ vs. SCE indicates active cathodic protection; Pathak et al., 2012).
  * *Self-Passivation vs. Sacrificial Current:* Zinc coatings also provide a physical barrier and **"self-healing"** as sparingly soluble $\text{Zn}$ corrosion products migrate to scratches, stopping under-film rust creep across several millimeters in air and farther in seawater (Nordic Galvanizers; Gorejová et al., 2021). Aluminum flake pigments form interlocking barrier scales, while silica-coated $\text{Al}$ pigments add a passivating silica-cation film (USBR, 2017). On structural $\text{Al}$, an adherent oxide with hydrated outer material slows self-corrosion, but on a sacrificial $\text{Al}$ electrode, strong passivation blocks protective current (Howell, 1999/2000).
  * *Alloy Activation and Capacities:* Because pure $\text{Al}$ passivates where $\text{Cl}^- < 1,446\text{ ppm}$ (Wikipedia Galvanic anode), **activation by $\text{In, Ga}$, or $\text{Sn}$ (e.g., `KA90` $\text{Al–Zn–Sn}$)**—replacing toxic $\text{Hg}$—disrupts the passive film for controlled pitting dissolution (US Navy MIL-DTL-24779D(SH), 2015, Table II; Genescá et al.). Adding $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3, \text{ZnO}, \text{MnO}_2$, or $\text{CeO}_2$ inclusions into composite $\text{Al–Zn}$ anodes improves dissolution uniformity and efficiency (Solovyeva et al., 2023). In seawater, $\text{Al}$ anodes provide **$2,000\text{ Ah/kg}$** vs. **$780\text{ Ah/kg}$** for $\text{Zn}$; by $\text{Anode Mass (kg)} = [\text{Current (A)} \times \text{Life (yr)} \times 8,760] / [\text{Utilisation Factor} \times \text{Capacity (Ah/kg)}]$, $\text{Al}$ requires only $780 \div 2,000 \approx 0.39$ times the mass of $\text{Zn}$ (Wikipedia Galvanic anode; iron anodes also galvanically protect copper).
* **Selective Cathodic Precipitation (Divalent and Rare-Earth Cations):** Soluble cations ($\text{ZnCl}_2, \text{ZnSO}_4\cdot 7\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{CeCl}_3, \text{Ce(NO}_3)_3$) migrate to cathodes where $\text{O}_2$ reduction raises local pH by $1\text{–}2$ units (Veolia, Ch. 24; Yesu Thangam et al., 2009). At $1.0\times 10^{-3}\text{ mol/L}$, **$\text{Zn(OH)}_2$ precipitates at $\text{pH } 7.24$** ($K_{\text{sp}} = 3.0\times 10^{-17}$), vs. $\text{pH } 4.00$ for $\text{Al(OH)}_3$ ($K_{\text{sp}} = 1.0\times 10^{-33}$) and $\text{pH } 9.87$ for $\text{Mg(OH)}_2$ ($K_{\text{sp}} = 5.6\times 10^{-12}$). Thus, $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ stays soluble in bulk cooling water ($\text{pH } 6.5\text{–}9.0$) without fouling, plating out selectively as $\text{Zn(OH)}_2, \text{ZnCO}_3$, or $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ at cathodes (Veolia, Ch. 24; Chen & Yang, 2019). Similarly, **rare-earth salts** ($\text{Ce, La, Y}$) precipitate poorly soluble $\text{Ce(OH)}_3\text{(s)}$ (oxidizing to mixed $\text{Ce}_2\text{O}_3/\text{Ce(OH)}_3/\text{CeO}_2$) or $\text{La(OH)}_3$ when cathodic $\text{OH}^-$ raises local $\text{pH} > 10$ above bulk $\text{pH } 6.5\text{–}7.0$, blocking cathodic intermetallics on AA7075, AA2024-T3, Al-Li, and galvanized/carbon steel (Hinton & Arnott, 1986; Arenas et al., 2003; Davó & de Damborenea, 2004; Mishra et al., 2007; Girčienė et al., 2015; Qian et al., 2015; Saji, 2019; Abdellah et al., 2021; Mendez et al., 2022).
* **Dissolved Sulfide ($\text{H}_2\text{S}$) Scavenging:** In sour drilling muds, $\text{Zn}$- and $\text{Fe}$-based scavengers precipitate thermally stable metal sulfides, having displaced $\text{Cu}$-based scavengers in the 1970s because noble $\text{Cu}^{2+}$ plates onto steel drillpipe and drives galvanic corrosion (Onaizi et al., 2020).
* **Autonomous $\text{Li–Al LDH}$ Growth and Smart Nanocontainer Ion Exchange:** In $\text{Li}$-leaching primers ($\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3, \text{Li}_2\text{C}_2\text{O}_4$, $\text{Li}$-polyurethane) on $\text{Al}$ alloys (AA2024-T3, AA7075, AA5083, AA6014, AA2198-T8), leached $\\text{Li}^+$ and $\\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ alkalinize scratches so anodic $\text{Al(OH)}_4^-$ co-precipitates with $\text{Li}^+$ into **a $0.2\text{–}1.5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ trilayer barrier** (compact inner, porous middle, and columnar outer **$\text{Li–Al LDH}$ + pseudoboehmite**; Liu et al., 2015; Visser et al., 2017, 2019; Gharbi et al., 2018; Michailidou et al., 2022). In pre-synthesized **$\text{LDHs}$ ($[ \text{M(II)}_{1-x}\text{M(III)}_x(\text{OH})_2 ]^{x+}(\text{A}^{n-})_{x/n}\cdot m\text{H}_2\text{O}, x = 0.22\text{–}0.33$)** or zinc layered hydroxide salts intercalated with $\text{A}^{n-} = \text{V}_2\text{O}_7^{4-}, \text{MoO}_4^{2-}, \text{NO}_3^-, \text{NO}_2^-, \text{PO}_4^{3-}$, ingressing $\text{Cl}^-$ triggers dual-action ion exchange: trapping $\text{Cl}^-$ while releasing inhibitor on demand (Buchheit & Mahajanam, 2007; Leal & Wypych, 2020; Tedim et al., 2022). *In-situ* synchrotron XRD fitted to the Avrami–Erofeyev model ($\alpha(t) = 1 - \exp[-k(t-t_0)^m]$) proves 2D diffusion-controlled $\text{NO}_3^-$ release, and on AA2024-T3 with $\text{Zn–Al LDH-VO}_x$, **$\sim 50\%$ of vanadate exchanges for $\text{Cl}^-$ in $2\text{ weeks}$ and $90\%$ in $1\text{ month}$**, exceeding chromate primer impedance (Tedim et al., 2022).
* **Anodic Passivation by Oxyanions, Nitrites, Nitrates, and Borates:**
  * *Oxidizing anions ($\text{CrO}_4^{2-}, \text{NO}_2^-, \text{NO}_3^-$):* Reduce directly on bare steel without $\text{O}_2$ (Palanisamy, 2019). Nitrite oxidizes $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ into a $2\text{ nm}$ **$\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 / \gamma\text{-FeOOH}$ n-type semiconductor film** while reducing to $\text{NO}$ (detected by XPS at the outer surface; Kim et al., 2015; Al-Refaie et al., 2010; Song et al., 2019):
    $$2\text{Fe}^{2+} + 2\text{OH}^- + 2\text{NO}_2^- \longrightarrow 2\text{NO}\uparrow + \text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \quad (\text{and } \text{Fe}^{2+} + \text{OH}^- + \text{NO}_2^- \longrightarrow \text{NO}\uparrow + \gamma\text{-FeOOH})$$
  * *Non-oxidizing Group VI oxyanions ($\text{MoO}_4^{2-}, \text{WO}_4^{2-}$):* Require dissolved $\text{O}_2$ via a substrate-dependent two-stage redox/competitive-adsorption mechanism (Al-Refaie et al., 2010; Lopez-Garrity & Frankel, 2014). On mild steel, $\text{MoO}_4^{2-}$ and $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ form soluble non-protective $\text{FeMoO}_4$, which $\text{O}_2$ oxidizes to an inner Fe-oxide/hydroxide and outer insoluble ferric molybdate ($\text{Fe}_2(\text{MoO}_4)_3$, or mixed $\text{Fe}_2(\text{WO}_4)_3 + \text{Fe}_2(\text{MoO}_4)_3$ with tungstate; Yang et al., 2022) that plugs $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ pores and electrostatically repels $\text{Cl}^-$ and $\text{HCO}_3^-/\text{CO}_3^{2-}$ (Tan et al., 2017; Kumar, 2020; Veolia, Ch. 24). On AA2024-T3, $\text{Na}_2\text{MoO}_4$ is an **$\text{O}_2$-dependent mixed inhibitor** over intermetallic particles (Lopez-Garrity & Frankel, 2014); on AA6063 in $\text{NaCl}$, it deposits a mixed-valence **$\text{Mo(VI, V, IV)}$ layer** over $\text{Fe}$-rich cathodic intermetallics ($\approx 90\%$ efficiency at $4\text{ h}$, $\approx 70\%$ at $1\text{ week}$; Kharitonov et al., 2020). In strong acid ($\text{pH} \le 1$, $1\text{ N H}_2\text{SO}_4$), $\text{Na}_2\text{MoO}_4$ polymerizes to $\text{Mo}_7\text{O}_{24}^{6-}$ and $\text{Mo}_8\text{O}_{26}^{4-}$; partial reduction of $2\text{–}4$ $\text{Mo}^{6+}$ ($E_b = 233.2\text{ eV}$) to $\text{Mo}^{5+}$ ($232.2\text{ eV}$) raises Keggin anion charge from $-3$ to $-5/-7$, anchoring it in **an outer $1.0 \pm 0.5\text{ nm}$ $\text{CrOOH}/\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ layer over a $2.0 \pm 0.5\text{ nm}$ $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3/\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4$ barrier** on stainless steel (Kazansky et al., 2013).
  * *Borates ($\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$):* Buffer pH and facilitate $\text{O}_2$ absorption on steel, though borate passive films ($\text{pH } 8.4$) dissolve completely upon cathodic reduction unlike phosphate films (Rio Tinto Borax; Sieber & Oudar, 2006).
  * *Nitrate vs. Nitrite Divergence in Alkaline Pore Water:* Though $\text{NO}_3^-$ has sluggish kinetics in neutral water, in alkaline concrete pore solution ($\text{pH } 12.5\text{–}13.5$) + $3.5\text{ wt\% NaCl}$, **$\text{Ca(NO}_3)_2$ and $\text{NaNO}_3$ achieve lower $I_{\text{corr}}$ than equimolar $\text{Ca(NO}_2)_2$ and $\text{NaNO}_2$** because $1\text{ mol NO}_3^-$ reduces via $\text{NO}_2^-$ to oxidize $3\text{ mol Fe}^{2+} \rightarrow \text{Fe}^{3+}$, whereas $1\text{ mol NO}_2^-$ oxidizes only $1\text{ mol Fe}^{2+}$ (Kumar, 2020; Elsener in Rivetti et al., 2018).

### 1.2 Specific Compounds and Subcategories

1. **Sacrificial Metals, Alloy Anodes, Pigments, and Soluble Divalent/Alkali Salts:** **$\text{Mg}$**, **$\text{Mg–Mn}$**, **$\text{Mg}$-rich primers** (for high-resistivity soils/freshwater, though losing $\approx 7.9\text{ kg/A}\cdot\text{yr}$ or $35\%\text{–}50\%$ mass via the anodic Negative Difference Effect [NDE], forming $\text{Mg(OH)}_2 \rightarrow \text{MgCO}_3$; US Army TM 5-811-7, 1985; Song & Atrens, 2003; Pathak et al., 2012; Hahn et al., 2015); **$\text{Zn}$**, **ASTM B418 Type I/II ($95\%\text{ Zn}$)**, **hot-dip galvanizing**, **zinc dust** (SeaZinc; Pais & Rao, 2021); **activated $\text{Al}$ alloys ($\text{Al–Zn–In}, \text{Al–Zn–Ga}, \text{Al–Zn–Sn}$ [`KA90`], $\text{Al–Zn–In–Mg–Ti–Mn}$)** per MIL-DTL-24779D(SH) (2015) and DNV-RP-B401 (2010/2011), **oxide-composite $\text{Al–Zn}$ anodes**, and **barrier/silica-coated $\text{Al}$ flakes** (Solovyeva et al., 2023; USBR, 2017); soluble **$\text{ZnCl}_2$**, **$\text{ZnSO}_4 / \text{ZnSO}_4\cdot 7\text{H}_2\text{O}$**, **$\text{ZnCO}_3 \cdot \text{Zn(OH)}_2$**, **$\text{MgSO}_4$**, **$\text{Mg(HCO}_3)_2$**, **$\text{NiSO}_4$**, and **$\text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3 / \text{Li}_2\text{C}_2\text{O}_4$** (Veolia, Ch. 24; Chen & Yang, 2019; Gogoi & Barhai, 2011; Visser et al., 2019).
2. **Rare-Earth Metal Salts:** **$\text{CeCl}_3$**, **$\text{Ce(NO}_3)_3$**, **$\text{Ce}_2(\text{SO}_4)_3$**, **cerium tartrate**, **$(\text{NH}_4)_2\text{Ce(NO}_3)_6$ ($\text{ACN}$)**, **$\text{La(NO}_3)_3$**, and **yttrium salts** (Hinton & Arnott, 1986; Bogan et al., 2004; Girčienė et al., 2015; Qian et al., 2015; Chen et al., 2022; Mendez et al., 2022).
3. **Transition-Metal Oxyanion Salts:** Legacy **$\text{Na}_2\text{CrO}_4, \text{Na}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{K}_2\text{CrO}_4, \text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7, \text{ZnCrO}_4, \text{SrCrO}_4$**, and **chrome lignites/lignosulfonates** (API/GOT Bul D11; Talbot & Talbot); low-toxicity **$\text{Na}_2\text{MoO}_4, (\text{NH}_4)_2\text{MoO}_4, \text{Li}_2\text{MoO}_4, \text{ZnMoO}_4, \text{Na}_2\text{WO}_4, \text{NaVO}_3$**, and **$\text{Mo}_7\text{O}_{24}^{6-} / \text{Mo}_8\text{O}_{26}^{4-}$** (Raman, 1996; Kazansky et al., 2013; USBR, 2017; Chen & Yang, 2019; UFC 3-230-13, 2023).
4. **Nitrites, Nitrates, Borates, Thiocyanates, Scavengers, and LDHs:** **$\text{Ca(NO}_2)_2, \text{NaNO}_2, \text{LiNO}_2, \text{Ca(NO}_3)_2, \text{NaNO}_3, \text{LiNO}_3, \text{Bi(NO}_3)_3$**; **borax ($\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**, **$\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7$**, **$\text{H}_3\text{BO}_3$**; **$\text{NaSCN}, \text{NH}_4\text{SCN}$, $\text{Zn}^{2+}+\text{SCN}^-$** (US Patent 4,728,446A), **$\text{KI}$**; oxygen scavengers **$\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_3, \text{NaHSO}_3/\text{NH}_4\text{HSO}_3, \text{SO}_2, \text{N}_2\text{H}_4$**; volatile **$\text{DICHAN}$**; and **$\text{Zn–Al}, \text{Ca–Al}, \text{Mg–Al}$ LDHs** or $\text{Zn}$ layered hydroxides with $\text{NO}_3^-, \text{NO}_2^-, \text{MoO}_4^{2-}, \text{V}_2\text{O}_7^{4-}$ (Ikeda et al., 1992; Bolzoni et al., 2014; Leal & Wypych, 2020; Fida et al., 2021; Hu et al., 2022; Tedim et al., 2022).

### 1.3 Typical Application Industries, Operating Windows, and Failure Modes

* **Sacrificial Cathodic Protection:** Galvanized steel ($>100\text{ yr}$ atmospheric life), $\text{Zn}$-rich bridge primers, $\text{Mg}$ anodes in high-resistivity soils/freshwater, and $\text{Al}$ (`KA90`, $\text{Al–Zn–In}$) or $\text{Zn}$ anodes in seawater on hulls, submarines, offshore platforms, heat exchangers, and tanks (DNV-RP-B401, 2010/2011 prefers $\text{Al}$ for capacity, noting $\text{Zn}$'s reliability in anaerobic marine sediments/bacterial compartments rather than $\text{Mg}$). *Failure modes:* (1) **Overprotection** from $\text{Mg}$'s negative potential drives $2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2e^- \rightarrow 2\text{H}\cdot + 2\text{OH}^-$, causing high-strength steel hydrogen embrittlement, coating cathodic disbondment, and amphoteric metal alkaline attack (avoided by $\text{Zn}$'s lower driving voltage; UFC 3-570-01, 2016/2019); (2) **$\text{Zn}$ high-temperature passivation** and potential reversal in hot water; (3) **$\text{Al}$ passivation at $\text{Cl}^- < 1,446\text{ ppm}$** and **thermite spark hazards** when striking rusty steel in explosive tanks (Wikipedia Galvanic anode); and (4) loss of electrical/ionic continuity (bolting $\text{Zn}$ to steel in air protects only across a few mm of moisture; US Army TM 5-811-7, 1985; Nordic Galvanizers).
* **Cooling Water Systems, Closed Loops, and Nuclear Primary Coolant:**
  * *Open/Closed Loops:* Legacy cooling towers used $6:1\text{ to }8:1$ $\text{CrO}_4^{2-}:\text{Zn}^{2+}$ ($10\text{–}25\text{ ppm}$ chromate) or $1:1$ ultralow-chrome ($3\text{–}8\text{ ppm}$) at $\text{pH } 6.0\text{–}7.0$ via $35\text{–}50\text{ ppm}$ of a $50\text{ wt\% }\text{Na}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7\cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 11\text{ wt\% }\text{ZnCl}_2 + 8\text{ wt\% }\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + 14\text{ wt\% HEDP}$ concentrate (Chen & Yang, 2019). Chromate-free programs use **alkaline zinc** ($5\text{ wt\% }\text{ZnCl}_2 + 10\text{ wt\% }\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 + 5\text{ wt\% HEDP}$ at $\text{pH } 7.0\text{–}9.0$ for low-hardness water; Veolia, Ch. 24), **refinery $\text{ZnSO}_4\cdot 7\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4 + \text{HEDP} + \text{diethyldithiocarbamate}$ blends** (Gogoi & Barhai, 2011), or **$\text{Na}_2\text{MoO}_4$ programs** ($16\text{–}25\text{ wt\%}$ concentrate at $75\text{–}125\text{ ppm}$ maintaining **$2.5\text{–}5.0\text{ ppm Mo}$ in open loops** and **$8\text{–}10\text{ ppm Mo}$ in closed chilled/glycol loops** at $\text{pH } 7.5\text{–}9.0$ in **molybdate–azole programs**; Chen & Yang, 2019; UFC 3-230-13, 2023, §6-2.2). In dam water ($\text{pH } 8.25, 10\text{ ppm Cl}^-$), **$250\text{ ppm Na}_2\text{MoO}_4 + 30\text{ ppm Zn}^{2+}$** achieved **$82\%$ ($7\text{ d}$) and $96\%$ ($10\text{ d}$)** mass-loss efficiency ($\approx 77\%$ by polarization; FTIR-verified Fe–molybdate + $\text{Zn(OH)}_2$ film; Yesu Thangam et al., 2009).
  * *Closed Loops, Municipal Scale, and Nuclear Coolant:* Closed loops and phosphate-free antifreeze use **nitrite–borate blends** ($30\text{ wt\% }\text{NaNO}_2 + 6\text{ wt\%}$ borax, US Patent 5,647,211; nitrites are restricted from open towers due to bacterial oxidation to $\text{NO}_3^-$, Veolia, Ch. 24), $\text{Li}_2\text{MoO}_4$ (absorption chillers), and **$\text{Na}_2\text{MoO}_4 + \text{BTA}$** (outperforming $\text{Na}_2\text{CrO}_4$ in 2022 EIS tests; Guo et al., 2022). Municipal distribution systems adjust pH/alkalinity via lime ($17\%$), soda ash, or $\text{NaOH}$ ($\sim 48\%$) across $46\%$ of surveyed US utilities for $\text{CaCO}_3$ scale, though the Langelier Saturation Index (LSI) was deprecated by USEPA in 2016 (US EPA, 2016; Hazen & Sawyer, 2022). In simulated nuclear primary coolant ($1.6\text{ ppm NaCl}, \text{pH } 9 \pm 0.2$), **ductile cast iron requires $\sim 100\times$ more $\text{NaNO}_2$ than carbon steel** due to graphite–iron galvanic cells and $\text{NO}$ adsorption on graphite (Kim et al., 2015).
* **Reinforced Concrete Structures:** **$\text{Ca(NO}_2)_2$** (*Sika CNI, BASF MasterLife CI30, W. R. Grace DCI, Euclid EUCON BCN*; FHWA, 1998, Ch. 5) is dosed at $0\text{–}30\text{ L/m}^3$ ($7.5\text{–}20\text{ kg/m}^3$ of $30\text{ wt\%}$ solution) or surface-applied ($35\%$ solution at $250\text{–}1,500\text{ g/m}^2$), yielding $92.30\%$ efficiency at $25\text{ g/L}$ in carbonated concrete ($1.66\%\text{ NO}_2^-$ by cement mass totally inhibits carbonation corrosion) and $64.90\%\text{–}85.75\%$ at $8.94\text{–}71.55\text{ g/L}$ in $\text{Cl}^-$ concrete (Rivetti et al., 2018; Song et al., 2019). Critical molar ratio $n(\text{NO}_2^-)/n(\text{Cl}^-)$: Song et al. (2019) report **$<0.4$** accelerates macro-cell pitting, **$0.8$** eliminates pitting, and **$>1.2$** fully suppresses corrosion (tripling under combined carbonation $+\text{Cl}^-$), while Bolzoni et al. (2014), Timofeeva (2017), and Bastidas et al. (2021) place the threshold at **$0.5\text{–}1.0$** ($0.5\text{–}0.6$ typical). Across $0, 10, 20, 30\text{ L/m}^3$ $\text{Ca(NO}_2)_2$, initiation $\text{NaCl}$ levels rose from $0.14\%$ to $0.26\%, 0.38\%, 0.46\%$ ($+0.32$ percentage points total), delaying initiation from $70$ to $125, 190, 235\text{ days}$ ($\approx 3.4\times$; Song et al., 2019, Table 1). Over 15 years, $\text{Ca(NO}_2)_2$ cut $\text{Cl}^-$ diffusivity from $6.8\text{–}10 \times 10^{-8}$ to $3.1\text{–}6.1 \times 10^{-8}\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}$ and **doubled the critical $\text{Cl}^-$ threshold from $0.5\%\text{–}1.2\%$ to $1.0\%\text{–}2.3\%$ cement weight** (passive at 5 yr at $0.8\text{ wt\% Cl}^-$ [ratio $0.55$], corroding at $1.2\text{ wt\% Cl}^-$ [ratio $0.37$]; leaching in uncracked immersion was $0.0041\%$ after $10\text{ months}$), but surface-applied migrating inhibitors (MCIs, $250\text{–}400\text{ g/m}^2$) penetrated **$\le 20\text{ mm}$** due to pore clogging, leaving active corrosion $>10\text{ }\mu\text{m/yr}$ (vs. $1\text{ }\mu\text{m/yr}$ passivity limit; Bolzoni et al., 2014). Unlike $\text{NaNO}_2$ ($82.60\%\text{–}95.00\%$ efficiency, which lowers compressive strength and accelerates alkali–aggregate reaction [AAR]), $\text{Ca(NO}_2)_2$ boosts early strength without AAR but accelerates setting/shrinkage (Song et al., 2019). In $3\text{ nm}$ $\text{C-S-H}$ nanopore MD simulations ($1\text{ M}, 300\text{ K}, 3,000\text{ ps}$), **$\text{LiNO}_2$ transports $\text{NO}_2^-$ fastest ($\sim 115\text{ \AA}$)** vs. $\text{Ca(NO}_2)_2/\text{NaNO}_2$ due to stronger water H-bond drag ($CN_{\text{NO}_2^--\text{H}_w} = 5.26$ vs. $4.38/4.27$) and weakest cation/wall pinning ($CN_{\text{Li}^+-\text{NO}_2^-} = 0.48, CN_{\text{Li}^+-\text{O}_w} = 0$ vs. $1.807/3.9$ for $\text{Na}^+$ and $2.28/5.6$ for $\text{Ca}^{2+}$; Hu et al., 2022). Also, $\text{Na}_2\text{MoO}_4$ gives $82.60\%\text{–}98.60\%$ efficiency ($1\text{–}40\text{ g/L}$; Rivetti et al., 2018), and **$400\text{ ppm}$ Ca-lignosulfonate $+ 600\text{ ppm Na}_2\text{MoO}_4$** in carbonated pore solution ($\text{pH } 11.5$) shifts pitting potential $+200\text{ mV}$ with $92.67\% \rightarrow 99.2\%$ ($24\text{ h}$) efficiency (Lin & Zuo, 2019).
* **Oil and Gas Production, Drilling Muds, Completion Brines, and Souring/MIC:**
  * *Scavengers & Sequential Injection:* Water-based muds use **zinc basic carbonate** ($\sim 0.1\text{ lbm/bbl}$ per $50\text{ mg/L}$ sulfide; $6.0\text{–}14.0\text{ kg/m}^3$; Schlumberger Glossary); oil-based muds use **$\text{ZnO}$** (Onaizi et al., 2020); and wells use nitrites for $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ and **$\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_3, \text{NaHSO}_3/\text{NH}_4\text{HSO}_3, \text{SO}_2$** for $\text{O}_2$ where anaerobic organic filmers fail (Obeyesekere et al., 2015; Solovyeva et al., 2023). However, excess bisulfite feeds sulfate-reducing microbes (SRM), suffers microbial oxidation, and **chemically corrodes carbon steel at $0.28 \pm 0.18\text{ mm/yr}$**—remedied by **sequentially injecting $\text{NO}_3^-$ *after* the bisulfite–$\text{O}_2$ reaction completes** (Fida et al., 2021). Sweet ($\text{CO}_2$) wells form protective *in-situ* **$\text{FeCO}_3$ (siderite)** only at high temperature/$\text{CO}_2$ pressure, remaining porous at $40\text{–}60\,^\circ\text{C}$ (Fazal et al., 2022).
  * *Completion Brines & the General Corrosion vs. SCC Dichotomy:* Heavy brines span **$10.02\text{ PPG}$ ($\text{NaCl}$) to $22.13\text{ PPG}$ ($\text{ZnBr}_2$)**, where $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ hydrolysis lowers pH from $4.92$ ($17.0\text{ PPG}$) to $3.04$ ($18.2\text{ PPG}$), driving severe general corrosion (Obeyesekere et al., 2015). **$\text{NaSCN}/\text{NH}_4\text{SCN}$, $\text{Zn}^{2+}+\text{SCN}^-$, and $\text{SCN}^-/\text{I}^-$ (`IOC`) + $\text{NH}_4\text{HSO}_3$** protect $\text{ZnBr}_2$ brines (where $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ traps sulfide as $\text{ZnS}$), but in **non-zinc brines ($\text{CaCl}_2, \text{CaBr}_2, \text{NaBr}$), $\text{SCN}^-$ thermally decomposes at $300\,^\circ\text{F}$ ($149\,^\circ\text{C}$) into free $\text{H}_2\text{S}$, causing catastrophic sulfide stress corrosion cracking (SCC)** of $13\text{Cr}$ and $22\text{Cr}$ duplex tubing (Ikeda et al., 1992; Mack et al., 2002; Henke & Carpenter, 2004; Skogsberg et al., 2013). Crucially, McKennis & Carter (2005) proved **general corrosion and SCC vary independently**: acidic $\text{ZnBr}_2$ resists cracking at $300\text{–}400\,^\circ\text{F}$, whereas $\text{CaCl}_2$ and thiocyanate-free $\text{NaBr}/\text{CaBr}_2$ suffer SCC at $300\,^\circ\text{F}$.
  * *Reservoir Souring & MIC Counterexamples:* Nitrate ($\text{NO}_3^-$) suppressed SRB activity and corrosion over $7\text{–}8\text{ yr}$ at North Sea **Veslefrikk and Gullfaks** fields (Bødtker et al., 2008). At **$80\,^\circ\text{C}$ Halfdan**, **$65\text{ mg/L NO}_3^-$ cut produced sulfide by $98\%$** ($5\text{ mg/L}$ [$0.2\text{ mM}$] at untreated Dan $\rightarrow 0.1\text{ mg/L}$ [$0.003\text{ mM}$]), suppressed $60\,^\circ\text{C}$ sulfate reduction from $20.9\text{–}31.8$ to $<0.01\text{ nmol}\cdot\text{mL}^{-1}\cdot\text{d}^{-1}$, cut total sulfate-reducing prokaryotes (SRP) by $77.9\%$ ($14.5\%$ [$14.1\%$ *Archaeoglobus*, $0.4\%$ bacterial] $\rightarrow 3.2\%$ [$2.9\%$ archaeal, $0.3\%$ bacterial] of 16S rRNA copies), raised $\text{NH}_4^+$ by $54.5\%$ ($33 \rightarrow 51\text{ mg/L}$), and kept residual $\text{NO}_3^-/\text{NO}_2^- < 0.5\text{ mg/L}$ via hNRB biocompetition, NR-SOB sulfide oxidation, and $\text{NO}_2^-$ inhibition of dissimilatory sulfite reductase ($dsrAB$; Gittel et al., 2009; Fan, 2018). *Counterexamples:* Lahme et al. (2019) proved **$\text{NO}_2^-$ and zero-valent sulfur ($\text{S}^0$) metabolites** from *Sulfurimonas* sp. nitrate-driven sulfide oxidation severely corrode carbon steel (aggravated by $\text{FeS}$). In gas pipelines, $0.1\text{ wt\% NaNO}_3$ increased weight loss by stimulating denitrifiers; in *Comamonas denitrificans* assays, $5\text{ mM ACN}$ reduced resting-cell $\text{N}_2$ ($85.8\% \rightarrow 27.8\%$) but failed in growing cells ($82.1\% \rightarrow 71.3\%\text{ N}_2$), whereas **$5\text{ mM Bi(NO}_3)_3$ arrested both resting ($18.4\%\text{ N}_2$) and growing ($0.8\%\text{ N}_2$) denitrification** (Bogan et al., 2004). In 44-day coupon trials ($3.24\text{ mpy}$ control), single agents cut corrosion by $33.3\%$ ($15\text{ mM Na}_2\text{MoO}_4$ [$\text{SM}$], $2.16\text{ mpy}$), $50.0\%$ ($1\text{ mM ACN}$, $1.62\text{ mpy}$), $58.0\%$ ($5\text{ mM BES}$, $1.36\text{ mpy}$), and $79.0\%$ ($100\text{ mM}$ glutaraldehyde, $0.68\text{ mpy}$), but blends were antagonistic ($\text{SM}+\text{BES } 45.1\%, \text{SM}+\text{ACN } 29.9\%, \text{BES}+\text{ACN } 2.5\%, \text{SM}+\text{BES}+\text{ACN } 48.5\%$; Bogan et al., 2004).
* **Metal Surface Treatment Limits:** Rare-earth conversion films ($\text{CeCl}_3, \text{Ce(NO}_3)_3$ on AA7075, AA2024-T3, AZ91D $\text{Mg}$, carbon steel), $\text{NaVO}_3, \text{Na}_2\text{WO}_4, \text{Li}_2\text{CO}_3$, $\text{LDHs}$, and $\text{ZnMoO}_4$ pigments replace chromates (Hinton & Arnott, 1986; Qian et al., 2015; Visser et al., 2019; Tedim et al., 2022). *Limits:* $\text{ZnMoO}_4$ ages resins and emits toxic welding fumes (USBR, 2017; EPRI); $10^{-2}\text{ M Na}_2\text{MoO}_4$ on HDG steel fails at $\text{pH } 13$ and drops $<30\%$ at $\text{pH} \le 4$ (Timofeeva, 2017); $\text{Zn–Al LDHs}$ dissolve $\sim 40\%$ at $\text{pH} \ge 13$ (Tedim et al., 2022); rare-earth films dissolve in acid, require narrow crack-free deposition windows, underperform $\text{Cr(VI)}$ in polyaniline, and faced $\text{Ce}$ price swings ($\text{US\$4.35} \rightarrow \text{US\$100/kg}$, 2001–2012; Gharbi et al., 2018); and $\text{Cr(III)}$ coatings lack self-healing and can oxidize back to $\text{Cr(VI)}$ via peroxide (Gharbi et al., 2018).

## 2. Metal Oxide-Based Inhibitors

"Metal oxide-based inhibitors" span **in-situ grown passive oxide films** ($\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 / \gamma\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 / \text{AlOOH}$), **pre-formed oxide/hydroxide pigments, conversion layers, and ceramic nanofillers** ($\text{Pb}_3\text{O}_4$, spinel ferrites $\text{MFe}_2\text{O}_4$, $\text{ZnO}$, $\text{ZnO–NiO}$, $\text{CeO}_2 / \text{Ce}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{TiO}_2$, micaceous $\alpha\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3, \text{ZrO}_2, \text{Ta}_2\text{O}_5$, and acid-pickling $\text{As}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Sb}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Bi}_2\text{O}_3$), and **semiconductor oxide photoelectrodes** ($\text{ZnO/TiO}_2$ heterojunctions) (Veolia, Ch. 24; Sonawane et al., 2012; USBR, 2017; Zhang et al., 2019; Ibrahim et al., 2020; Al-Saadi & Singh Raman, 2022).

### 2.1 Basic Working Principle

1. **In-Situ Passive Oxide Growth vs. Chloride Breakdown:** Oxidants/$\text{O}_2$ grow $2\text{–}20\text{ nm}$ $\gamma\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3 / \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4$ passive films, but atomic-scale *in-situ* TEM on $\text{FeCr}_{15}\text{Ni}_{15}$ shows $\text{Cl}^-$ accumulates at the metal/film interface, causing lattice expansion and undulation that nucleate pits (Zhang et al., 2018).
2. **Spinel Ferrite Interfacial Alkalization and Saponification ($\text{MFe}_2\text{O}_4$ vs. Indirect $\text{Pb}_3\text{O}_4$):** **Red lead ($\text{Pb}_3\text{O}_4$)**, **$\text{PbO}$**, and **calcium plumbate** ($<0.001\text{ wt\%}$ water solubility) are *indirect* pigments that saponify linseed oil/alkyd fatty acids into lead soaps, which degrade in $\text{H}_2\text{O}/\text{O}_2$ into soluble short-chain carboxylates—**lead azelate ($\text{C}_9\text{H}_{16}\text{O}_4\text{Pb}$)**, **suberate ($\text{C}_8\text{H}_{14}\text{O}_4\text{Pb}$)**, and **pelargonate ($\text{C}_9\text{H}_{18}\text{O}_2\text{Pb}$)** (peaking at $\text{C}_9$)—that reinforce $\text{Fe(III)}$ oxide-hydroxide films (USBR, 2017; Gichuhi, 2021). Non-toxic **spinel ferrites ($\text{MFe}_2\text{O}_4$, $\text{M} = \text{Zn, Ca, Mg, Sr, Ba, Mn}$, or $\text{Ni}_{0.5}\text{Zn}_{0.5}\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_4$)** form soaps in oleoresinous binders *and* partially hydrolyze to create an **alkaline coating–substrate interface**; in soap-free **epoxy binders** ($3\text{ wt\% NaCl}$ EIS), all ferrites outperformed $\text{Pb}_3\text{O}_4$, albeit with a water-permeation **passivation lag time** (USBR, 2017; Emerald ACMM, 2017).
3. **Electron-Accepting Anodic $\text{ZnO}$, Composite Barriers, Buffering, and $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ Scavenging:** Unlike metallic $\text{Zn}$, $\text{ZnO}$ cannot supply galvanic electrons. Sonawane et al. (2012) proved **$\text{ZnO}$ nanoparticles act as an anodic inhibitor and nanofiller**, forming a passive layer with $\text{H}_2\text{O}/\text{O}_2$ that inhibits anodic-to-cathodic electron flow *"by accepting electrons from the metal"* more efficiently than $\text{Sb}_2\text{O}_3, \text{TiO}_2,$ or $\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3$. Low-loading $\text{ZnO}$-epoxy protects naval steel (Panaite & Mușat, 2014), and **$\text{ZnO–NiO}$-epoxy** enhances physical barrier protection on mild steel in $3.5\%\text{ NaCl}$ (Ibrahim et al., 2020). $\text{ZnO}$ also buffers acidic binder degradation products, releases cathodic $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ ($\text{ZnO} + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{Zn}^{2+} + 2\text{OH}^-$), and scavenges $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ ($\text{ZnO} + \text{H}_2\text{S} \rightarrow \text{ZnS} + \text{H}_2\text{O}$, **$419\text{ mg H}_2\text{S/g ZnO}$** theoretical capacity; Onaizi et al., 2020).
4. **Valence-Cycling $\text{CeO}_2 / \text{Ce}_2\text{O}_3$ vs. Film Morphology Limits:** $\text{CeO}_2$ undergoes $\text{Ce}^{4+}/\text{Ce}^{3+}$ redox cycling and precipitates cathodic $\text{Ce}_2\text{O}_3/\text{Ce(OH)}_3/\text{CeO}_2$ barriers (Qian et al., 2015; Saji, 2019). However, Girčienė et al. (2015) showed on carbon steel (over amorphous Fe-phosphate) that adding $\text{SO}_4^{2-}$ raised total $\text{Ce}$ and $\text{Ce(IV)}$ ($\text{CeO}_2$) content yet produced **thinner, more cracked, less protective films** than sulfate-free films—proving $\text{Ce(IV)}$ content cannot overcome cracked morphology. **Ce-doped $\text{ZnO}$** uses oxygen vacancies and variable-valence $\text{Ce}$ as a cathodic "double trap" (ACS Ind. Eng. Chem. Res., 2022), while **Zr-doped $\text{CeO}_2$** reinforces waterborne epoxies (ACS Appl. Nano Mater., 2021).
5. **Pure Barrier Tortuosity ($\text{TiO}_2$, Micaceous $\alpha\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$, ALD) and Photocathodic Protection ($\text{ZnO/TiO}_2$):** In dark service, **$\text{TiO}_2$** and lamellar **micaceous iron oxide ($\alpha\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$)** are physical **barrier pigments** (the most effective topcoat barriers over $\text{Zn}$-rich primers in $3\%\text{ NaCl}$; in $\text{TiO}_2\text{–CeO}_2$ hybrids, $\text{CeO}_2$ inhibits while $\text{TiO}_2$ is the matrix; Qian et al., 2015). $\text{TiO}_2, \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3, \text{ZrO}_2, \text{SiO}_2$ crosslink silanols ($\text{Fe–O–Si–O–M}$), and Atomic Layer Deposition (ALD) grows pinhole-free $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3, \text{TiO}_2, \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3\text{–Ta}_2\text{O}_5$ nanolaminates (Salmi, 2015; Dias et al., 2020). Under simulated sunlight, **$\text{ZnO/TiO}_2$ heterojunction photoelectrodes** transfer photoelectrons to cathodically polarize 316 SS and Q235 steel in $3.5\text{ wt\% NaCl}$, but require continuous illumination and consumable **$0.1\text{ M Na}_2\text{S} + 0.1\text{ M Na}_2\text{SO}_3$ hole scavengers** (Zhang et al., 2019).
6. **Cathodic Poisoning in Acids:** $\text{As}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Sb}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Bi}_2\text{O}_3$ plate metallically in acid pickling to poison $\text{H}_2$ evolution (Palanisamy, 2019).

### 2.2 Specific Compounds and Subcategories

1. **Spinel Ferrites ($\text{MFe}_2\text{O}_4$):** **$\text{ZnFe}_2\text{O}_4, \text{CaFe}_2\text{O}_4, \text{MgFe}_2\text{O}_4, \text{SrFe}_2\text{O}_4, \text{BaFe}_2\text{O}_4, \text{MnFe}_2\text{O}_4, \text{Ni}_{0.5}\text{Zn}_{0.5}\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_4$** (USBR, 2017; Emerald ACMM, 2017).
2. **Rare-Earth & Transition-Metal Oxides:** **$\text{CeO}_2$, $\text{Ce}_2\text{O}_3$, mixed $\text{Ce(III)/Ce(IV)}$ films, $\text{TiO}_2\text{–CeO}_2$, Zr-doped $\text{CeO}_2$, Ce-doped $\text{ZnO}$, $\text{Y}_2\text{O}_3$, $\text{Pr}_6\text{O}_{11}$, $\text{MoO}_2, \text{MoO}_3$** (Bogan et al., 2004; Girčienė et al., 2015; Qian et al., 2015; Ngo, 2019; Wan et al., 2022).
3. **Semiconducting, Heterojunction & Ceramic Oxides:** **$\text{ZnO}$, $\text{ZnO–NiO}$, $\text{ZnO}$ + hydroxyapatite, $\text{ZnO/TiO}_2$ photoelectrodes, $\text{TiO}_2, \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3, \text{ZrO}_2, \text{Ta}_2\text{O}_5$** (Sonawane et al., 2012; Salmi, 2015; Zhang et al., 2019; Dias et al., 2020; Ibrahim et al., 2020; Coatings, 2022).
4. **Iron Oxides & Bauxite Residue:** **Micaceous $\alpha\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$**, *in-situ* **$\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 / \gamma\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$**, and **red mud** ($\text{Fe}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3, \text{TiO}_2, \text{SiO}_2$; Dooley & Bursik, 2010; Qian et al., 2015; Rivetti et al., 2018).
5. **Legacy Lead & Acid-Poison Oxides:** **$\text{Pb}_3\text{O}_4$, $\text{PbO}$, $\text{Ca}_2\text{PbO}_4$, $\text{As}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Sb}_2\text{O}_3, \text{Bi}_2\text{O}_3$** (USBR, 2017; Palanisamy, 2019; Talbot & Talbot).

### 2.3 Typical Application Industries, Operating Windows, and Limitations

* **Coatings & Surface Treatments:** Across USBR dams (Grand Coulee, Parker, Flatiron, Crystal), **legacy $\text{Pb}_3\text{O}_4$ primers averaged $64\text{ years}$ service life** (1938 Parker Dam gates reached **$77\text{ years}$**) vs. $20\text{–}25\text{ years}$ for modern lead/chromate-free coatings (USBR, 2017, Table 1). Modern systems include $\text{MFe}_2\text{O}_4$ epoxies; **$5\text{ wt\% ZnO}$ nanocontainers in alkyd on mild steel** (cutting $I_{\text{corr}}$ by $77.5\%$ from **$0.00355\text{ to }0.0008\text{ A/cm}^2$**; Sonawane et al., 2012); $\text{ZnO}$ and $\text{ZnO–NiO}$ epoxies (Panaite & Mușat, 2014; Ibrahim et al., 2020); $\text{CeO}_2$ in waterborne acrylics and $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4$; **cerium tartrate in epoxy on AA2024-T3** (**$>520\text{ days}$** protection; Chen et al., 2022); $\text{Ce}^{3+}$-stearic acid films on Al 2024 (**$66\times$ boost** in $0.5\text{ M NaCl}$); **flash-PEO ceramic layers** with $\text{EDTA–Ce/Mo/W/V}$ (**$1,000\text{ h}$ salt spray**; del Olmo et al., 2020); $100\text{ nm}$ ALD $\text{TiO}_2/\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ on $\text{Al}$ (**$99\%$ enhancement**, $\text{Al-resin}+\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ beating $\text{Al-Al}_2\text{O}_3$ by $24.95\%$ and resin by $33.40\%$; Dias et al., 2020); $4.4\text{ nm}$ ALD $\text{TiO}_2$ tripling anode life to $80\text{ h}$ at $\text{pH} \approx 0$ (Trần-Phú et al., 2022); reactive-oxygen-generating $\text{ZnO/TiO}_2$ silanes against MIC (Al-Saadi & Singh Raman, 2022; Franco et al., 2022); and illuminated $\text{ZnO/TiO}_2$ photoelectrodes (Zhang et al., 2019).
* **Oil & Gas, Boilers, and Concrete:** Dry $\text{ZnO/TiO}_2$ nanoparticles adsorb $25.14\text{ mg H}_2\text{S/g}$ at $22\text{–}280\,^\circ\text{C}$ (Kumar, 2018), and $5\text{ mM MoO}_2, \text{MoO}_3, \text{Bi}_2\text{O}_3$ inhibit pipeline SRB/denitrifiers (Bogan et al., 2004). In water-based mud ($0.1\text{ wt\%}$, $\text{pH } 9.0\text{–}9.2$), bulk $\text{ZnO}$ reached only **$8.8\%$ saturation utilization** ($5.5\text{ mg/g}$ breakthrough, $35.1\text{ mg/g}$ saturation vs. $125.3/307.5\text{ mg/g}$ for $\text{KMnO}_4$ and $15.8/146.8\text{ mg/g}$ for $\text{Cu(NO}_3)_2$), but preserved mud rheology (apparent viscosity $14.9 \rightarrow 15.0\text{ cP}$, plastic viscosity $+2.7\%$), whereas at **$\text{pH} > 11$**, soluble zincate ($\text{Zn(OH)}_4^{2-}$) degrades rheology (Onaizi et al., 2020). High-pressure boilers rely on *in-situ* **$\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 / \gamma\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$** ($3\text{Fe} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4 + 4\text{H}_2$ at $\text{pH } 9.0\text{–}9.5, \text{O}_2 < 0.005\text{ ppm}$; Veolia, Ch. 11), while $\text{ZnO}$ and red mud protect rebar against Hausmann's $[ \text{Cl}^- ]/[ \text{OH}^- ] = 0.6$ pitting threshold (Rivetti et al., 2018).
* **Limitations:** Ferrite passivation lag time; amphoteric dissolution outside Pourbaix windows ($\text{pH } 4.0\text{–}8.5$ for $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$; $\text{pH } 6.0\text{–}12.0$ for $\text{ZnO}$); EU $\text{ZnO}$ aquatic-toxicity labeling; nanoparticle agglomeration without citrate/diazonium grafting (Ngo, 2019); sulfate-induced $\text{CeO}_2$ cracking (Girčienė et al., 2015); light/scavenger dependence of $\text{ZnO/TiO}_2$; and $\text{MoO}_3$ sublimation (Palanisamy, 2019).

## 3. Phosphate-Based Inhibitors

Phosphate inhibitors function across all sectors: orthophosphates as **$\text{O}_2$-dependent anodic passivators and cathodic precipitators** (Veolia, Ch. 24), polyphosphates as **cathodic colloidal film-formers and sequestrants** (Dariva & Galio, 2014; US EPA, 2016), monofluorophosphates as **alkaline-hydrolyzing pore-blockers/passivators** (Bastidas et al., 2021), and metal phosphates as **conversion coatings or slow-release pigments** (Simescu & Idrissi, 2008; Wan et al., 2017; Griffiths et al., 2022). *(Organic phosphonates like $\text{HEDP}$ are distinct from inorganic phosphates; Gogoi & Barhai, 2011.)*

### 3.1 Basic Working Principle

* **Dual Anodic–Cathodic Action in Water:** $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$ dissociates across $pK_{a1} = 2.15\text{–}2.23, pK_{a2} = 7.20\text{–}7.21, pK_{a3} = 12.32\text{–}12.35$ ($\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-$ at $\text{pH } 3\text{–}6$, $\text{HPO}_4^{2-}$ at $\text{pH } 8\text{–}12$, $\text{PO}_4^{3-}$ at $\text{pH} > 12$). Without $\text{O}_2$, iron remains active; with **dissolved $\text{O}_2$**, oxygen grows a $20\text{ nm}$ $\gamma\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ film while phosphate plugs pores with **strengite ($\text{FePO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}, K_{\text{sp}} = 1.3\times 10^{-22}, pK_{\text{sp}} = 26$ anhydrous)** and **vivianite ($\text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2 \cdot 8\text{H}_2\text{O}, K_{\text{sp}} = 1.0\times 10^{-36}, pK_{\text{sp}} = 32$)** (Veolia, Ch. 24; Yin, 2009; Bastidas et al., 2021). Simultaneously, $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{Zn}^{2+}$ orthophosphates precipitate at alkaline cathodes (requiring polymeric dispersants to prevent bulk $\text{Ca}$ scaling; Veolia, Ch. 24). In unbuffered perchlorate ($\text{pH } 9.6\text{–}11.6$), Armstrong et al. (1994) showed part of $\text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4$'s inhibition stems from solution alkalization.
* **Thermodynamic Competition and Duplex Films in Concrete:** Phosphates outcompete chlorides thermodynamically at $298.15\text{ K}$ (Bastidas et al., 2021, Eqs. 4–9):
  $$\text{Fe}^{2+} + 2\text{PO}_4^{3-} \rightleftharpoons \text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2 \; (\Delta G_f^\circ = -2444.80\text{ kJ/mol}) \quad \text{vs.} \quad \text{FeCl}_2 \; (-302.35\text{ kJ/mol})$$
  $$\text{Fe}^{3+} + \text{PO}_4^{3-} \rightleftharpoons \text{FePO}_4 \;\;\;\; (\Delta G_f^\circ = -1663.98\text{ kJ/mol}) \quad \text{vs.} \quad \text{FeCl}_3 \; (-668.11\text{ kJ/mol})$$
  Locking $\text{Fe}$ into phosphate ($4.44\%$ molar volume contraction) prevents autocatalytic pit acidification ($\text{Fe}^{2+} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons \text{Fe(OH)}_2 + 2\text{H}^+, \Delta G^\circ = +34.73\text{ kJ/mol}$). FTIR/XPS in $\text{Cl}^-$-contaminated pore solution confirms a **duplex film**: an inner solid-state Fe-(hydro)oxide layer and an outer dissolution–precipitation layer of **$\text{FeHPO}_4, \text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ ($pK_{\text{sp}} = 32$), and $\text{FePO}_4$ ($pK_{\text{sp}} = 26$)**, plus stabilized ferrihydrite ($\text{FeOOH}$) and pore $\text{Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ (Bastidas et al., 2021; Mohamed et al., 2022—*note: Mohamed et al. Table 2 prints $i_0 = 5.20$ and $i = 1.00\text{ }\mu\text{A/cm}^2$ at $0.60\text{ M Na}_3\text{PO}_4$ as $91.7\%$ efficiency, whereas $100(1 - i/i_0) = 80.8\%$*). At $[ \text{PO}_4^{3-} ]/[ \text{Cl}^- ] > 0.6$, $\text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4$ is a mixed inhibitor raising the stressed ($80\%$ UTS) $[ \text{Cl}^- ]/[ \text{OH}^- ]$ threshold from $0.4$ to $5.0$; below $0.6$, it is a cathodic precipitator (Bastidas et al., 2021).
* **Cathodic Colloidal Polyphosphates:** $\text{P}_2\text{O}_7^{4-}, \text{P}_3\text{O}_{10}^{5-}, (\text{PO}_3)_6^{6-}$ chelate $\text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{Mg}^{2+}, \text{Zn}^{2+}, \text{Fe}^{2+}$ into positively charged colloids that migrate electrophoretically to cathodes to form amorphous glassy films (reverting slowly to $\text{FePO}_4$; Dariva & Galio, 2014). In potable water, US EPA (2016) notes polyphosphates act mainly as $\text{Fe/Mn}$ sequestrants rather than $\text{Pb/Cu}$ passivators.
* **Zinc Phosphate Pigments and Conversion Baths:** Yin (2009) and USBR (2017) review five $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2 \cdot 2\text{–}4\text{H}_2\text{O}$ pigment models: (1) $\text{HPO}_4^{2-}$ release forming anodic phosphophyllite ($\text{Zn}_2\text{Fe(PO}_4)_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$); (2) $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ redeposition in scratches; (3) binder $-\text{COOH}/-\text{OH}$ zinc soaps; (4) Pryor's $\text{O}_2$-grown $\gamma\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$ plugged by $\text{FePO}_4$; and (5) Clay & Cox's cathodic basic-salt polarization. On scratched waterborne acrylic/steel in $3.5\%\text{ NaCl}$, $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ forms interfacial Fe-phosphate and improves wet adhesion against disbondment (Wan et al., 2017), while a 2022 SKP study proved **in-coating $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ is the primary active inhibitor against cathodic disbondment and $\text{PO}_4^{3-}$ mainly controls $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ solubility** (Griffiths et al., 2022). To overcome first-generation $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$'s low solubility lag at $\text{pH } 6.5\text{–}8.0$ without exceeding the $<2\text{ g/100 mL}$ blistering limit, second-generation pigments use **$\text{AlH}_2\text{P}_3\text{O}_{10} \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$** (chelated ferric triphosphate layer; USBR, 2017), $\text{ZPA}/\text{ZAPP}$ (Mirzakhanzadeh et al., 2018), $\text{BTA}$-modified $\text{ZnP}$ (Wint et al., 2020), and GRAS **$\text{Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ ("Hybrid A")** (Gichuhi, 2021). However, because phosphate films are only stable across **$\text{pH } 5\text{–}10$** (vs. $\text{pH } 2\text{–}12$ for $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3$) and lack redox self-healing, they cannot replace $\text{Cr(VI)}$ in aerospace conversion coatings (Gharbi et al., 2018). In **conversion baths**, micro-anodic iron dissolution ($\text{Fe} + 2\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4 \rightarrow \text{Fe(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2 + \text{H}_2\uparrow$) consumes acid and raises interfacial pH, crystallizing soluble primary $\text{Zn(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2$ into XRD-verified **hopeite ($\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$)** and **phosphophyllite ($\text{Zn}_2\text{Fe(PO}_4)_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$)** with $\text{NO}_2^-/\text{NO}_3^-$ accelerators (Simescu & Idrissi, 2008; Tamilselvi et al., 2015; Kiesow, 2016), whereas iron phosphating forms an **amorphous iron-phosphate/iron-oxide mixture** (Girčienė et al., 2015; Kiesow, 2016).

### 3.2 Specific Compounds and Subcategories

1. **Orthophosphates & Fluorophosphates:** **$\text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4$ (TSP)**, **$\text{Na}_2\text{HPO}_4$ (DHP)**, **$\text{NaH}_2\text{PO}_4$**, **$\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$**, **$\text{Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ ("Hybrid A")**, **hydrazine phosphate**, and **$\text{Na}_2\text{PO}_3\text{F}$ (MFP)** (Veolia, Ch. 11 & 24; Gichuhi, 2021; Bastidas et al., 2021).
2. **Polyphosphates & Synergistic Blends:** **$\text{TKPP}$ ($\text{K}_4\text{P}_2\text{O}_7$)**, **$\text{SHMP}$ ($(\text{NaPO}_3)_6$)**, **$\text{STPP}$ ($\text{Na}_5\text{P}_3\text{O}_{10}$)**, and $1:1$ **phosphonate–alkali gluconate blends** (US10094203B2, Zaid et al., 2018; Chen & Yang, 2019; US EPA, 2016).
3. **Metal Phosphates, Conversion Layers & Heteropolyphosphates:** **Hopeite ($\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**, **phosphophyllite ($\text{Zn}_2\text{Fe(PO}_4)_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**, **scholzite ($\text{Zn}_2\text{Ca(PO}_4)_2 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**, **liquid zinc orthophosphate ($\text{ZOP}$, `CP630`, $1:5\text{ Zn}:\text{PO}_4$)**, **$\text{Zn(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2$**, **vivianite ($\text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2 \cdot 8\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**, **strengite ($\text{FePO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$)**, **$\text{Mn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2 / \text{Mn}_5\text{H}_2(\text{PO}_4)_4$**, **$\text{Cu}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$**; second-generation **basic/modified $\text{ZnP}$, $\text{BTA}$-$\text{ZnP}$, $\text{ZPA}/\text{ZAPP}$, $\text{AlH}_2\text{P}_3\text{O}_{10} \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$, $\text{Zn}$ molybdate phosphate, $\text{Zn}$ silicophosphate, $\text{Sr–Zn}$ phosphosilicate**; and Keggin **$\text{H}_3\text{PMo}_{12}\text{O}_{40} \cdot 15\text{H}_2\text{O}$** and **$\text{H}_3\text{PW}_{12}\text{O}_{40} \cdot 17\text{H}_2\text{O}$** (Guan, 2007; Yin, 2009; Kazansky et al., 2013; Kiesow, 2016; USBR, 2017; Wint et al., 2020).

### 3.3 Typical Application Industries, Operating Windows, and the Boiler Hideout Reversal

* **Cooling Water and Municipal Potable Water:**
  * *Cooling Water:* Chen & Yang (2019, Tables 6–7) specify a **Stabilized Phosphate Program ($\text{pH } 6.5\text{–}7.5$)** at $2:1$ (hard) or $3:1$ (soft) ortho:poly ($\text{O:P}$) ratio with **$10\text{–}16\text{ ppm}$ $\text{O-PO}_4^{3-}$** (Product A: $40\text{ wt\% KOH}, 15\text{ wt\% H}_3\text{PO}_4, 15\text{ wt\% TKPP}, 7\text{ wt\% AA/AMPS}, 1\text{ wt\% TTA}$ stepped from $100 \rightarrow 60\text{–}65\text{ ppm}$ and Product B stabilizer [$15\text{ wt\% NaOH}, 10\text{ wt\% PCA}, 15\text{ wt\% PAA}, 15\text{ wt\% AA/AMPS}$] stepped from $30 \rightarrow 75\text{ ppm}$ as $\text{Ca}$ hardness rises from $300 \rightarrow 1,200\text{ ppm}$), and an **Alkaline Phosphate Program ($\text{pH } 8.0\text{–}9.0$)** at $1:2\text{ O:P}$ with $2\text{–}5\text{ ppm PO}_4^{3-}$ ($5\text{ wt\% H}_3\text{PO}_4 + 6\text{ wt\% TKPP} + 5\text{ wt\% HEDP} + 10\text{ wt\% SSMA}$). At $100\,^\circ\text{C}$, $80\text{ ppm Na}_2\text{HPO}_4 + \text{Na-gluconate}$ gives **$98.1\%$ efficiency ($0.014\text{ g/m}^2\cdot\text{d}$)** (Raheem, 2011), while phosphate alone beat tested silicate/phosphate mixtures in simulated cooling water (Anaee, 2013/2014).
  * *Potable Water:* In pipe loops ($\text{pH } 7.3\text{–}7.6$), **$\text{ZOP}$ (`CP630`, $0.5\text{–}2.0\text{ mg/L as P}$)** shifts scales from $\text{FeCO}_3$ to $\text{FePO}_4$, $\text{Cu}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$, and hydroxypyromorphite ($\text{Pb}_5(\text{PO}_4)_3\text{OH}$), keeping 90th-percentile $\text{Pb} < 0.005\text{ mg/L}$ and $\text{Cu} = 1.04\text{ mg/L}$ ($<1.3\text{ mg/L}$ EPA Action Level; Guan, 2007). In a Feb 2022 US utility survey (Hazen & Sawyer, 2022), **$\text{ZOP}$ was the #1 inhibitor**, followed by ortho- and blended ortho/polyphosphate. Per March 2016 USEPA guidance ($1\text{–}3\text{ mg/L as PO}_4$ at **$\text{pH } 7.2\text{–}7.8$**; $56\%$ of utilities in range, $36\%$ at $<1.0\text{ mg/L}$), 90th-percentile $\text{Cu}$ was lower with orthophosphate alone (**$0.10\text{ ppm}$**) than blended ortho/poly (**$0.32\text{ ppm}$**), though utilities flagged **increased wastewater phosphorus loads** under 2019 LCRR $1\text{–}3\text{ mg/L}$ rules (US EPA, 2016; Hazen & Sawyer, 2022).
* **Boiler Water Treatment — Buffering, Hideout APC, and Oxygen Scavengers:**
  * *Buffering vs. Hideout Reversal:* Phosphates protect boilers indirectly by conditioning $\text{Ca}$ hardness into blowdown hydroxyapatite sludge ($\text{Na}_2\text{HPO}_4/\text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4$; UFC 3-230-13, 2023, App. E) and buffering free $\text{NaOH}$ in **coordinated phosphate/pH control** (ASME feedwater $\text{pH } 8.3\text{–}10.5$; drum $\text{pH } 9.0\text{–}10.0$ at $5\text{–}10\text{ ppm PO}_4^{3-}$; $\text{NaNO}_3$ prevents caustic embrittlement; Veolia, Ch. 11; Chen & Yang, 2019):
    $$\text{Na}_2\text{HPO}_4 + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}; \quad \text{NaH}_2\text{PO}_4 + \text{NaOH} \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{HPO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O}; \quad \text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4 + \text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Na}_2\text{HPO}_4 + \text{NaOH}$$
    Historically, **Congruent Phosphate Treatment (CPT)** kept bulk $\text{Na}:\text{PO}_4 = 2.2:1\text{ to }2.6:1$. However, above **$10.3\text{ MPa}$ ($1,500\text{ psi}$) and $>250\,^\circ\text{C}$**, retrograde-solubility **phosphate hideout** precipitates incongruently under magnetite deposits at local $\text{Na}:\text{PO}_4 \le 2.0$, where $\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-$ fluxes $\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4$ into crystalline **maricite ($\text{NaFePO}_4$)**, causing rapid **Acid Phosphate Corrosion (APC)** tube gouging (Dooley & Bursik, 2010; IAPWS TGD4-11, 2015, §§4–6; Quinlan et al., 2015):
    $$\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4\text{(s)} + 3\text{Na}^+\text{(aq)} + 3\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)} \rightleftharpoons 3\text{NaFePO}_4\text{(s)} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$$
    Thus, **IAPWS (2015)** and Dooley & Bursik (2010) **prohibit acidic $\text{NaH}_2\text{PO}_4/\text{Na}_2\text{HPO}_4$ in high-pressure drum boilers/HRSGs**, mandating **exclusively $\text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4$ (TSP-only addition, $\text{Na}:\text{PO}_4 \ge 3.0:1$ + $\le 1\text{ mg/L}$ free $\text{NaOH}$, $\text{pH} \ge 9.0$, $0.2\text{–}0.3\text{ mg/L}$ min $\text{PO}_4^{3-}$)** so magnetite forms benign, redissolvable $\text{SIHP}$ ($\text{Na}_4\text{Fe(OH)(PO}_4)_2 \cdot \frac{1}{3}\text{NaOH}$ at $\text{Na}:\text{PO}_4 > 2.8$).
  * *Boiler Scavenger Kinetics (Veolia, Ch. 11):* Below $1,000\text{ psig}$, **$\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_3$** ($2\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_3 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_4$; $7.88\text{ ppm/ppm O}_2$ theoretical, $\sim 10\text{ lb/lb}$ practical, $>20\text{ ppm}$ residual) doubles in rate every $18\,^\circ\text{F}$ ($10\,^\circ\text{C}$), peaks at **$\text{pH } 8.5\text{–}10.0$**, and with $\text{Fe/Cu/Co/Ni/Mn}$ catalysts removes $100\%\text{ O}_2$ in $25\text{ s}$ (vs. $<50\%$ uncatalyzed). Above **$1,000\text{ psig}$**, sulfite decomposes to corrosive $\text{SO}_2/\text{H}_2\text{S}$, requiring **$\text{N}_2\text{H}_4$** ($\text{N}_2\text{H}_4 + \text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + \text{N}_2$, $1.5\text{–}2.0\text{ parts/part O}_2$ at $\text{pH } 9.0\text{–}10.0$), which decomposes at $400\text{–}600\,^\circ\text{F}$ ($2\text{N}_2\text{H}_4 \rightarrow \text{N}_2 + \text{H}_2 + 2\text{NH}_3$, attacking $\text{Cu}$ alloys).
* **Concrete Reinforcement — Pretreatment, MFP Reactions, and Cover Immobilization Limits:** Immersing rebar in **$0.5\text{ M Na}_3\text{PO}_4$** raises $\text{Cl}^-$ threshold from $0.1$ to $0.3\text{ M}$ (Manera et al., 2008); on zinc-phosphated rebar, pore-water $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ slows alkaline coating dissolution via hydroxyapatite-like products (Simescu & Idrissi, 2008); and admixed $\text{Na}$-phosphate effectiveness declines after 2 yr (Materials & Structures, 2000). For **$\text{Na}_2\text{PO}_3\text{F}$ (MFP)** in OPC mortar ($\text{pH} > 12$), $\text{PO}_3\text{F}^{2-}$ hydrolyzes ($\text{PO}_3\text{F}^{2-} + 2\text{OH}^- \rightleftharpoons \text{PO}_4^{3-} + \text{F}^- + \text{H}_2\text{O}, \Delta G^\circ = -54.34\text{ kJ/mol}, K_{\text{eq}} = 8.772 \times 10^7$) and reacts with $\text{Ca(OH)}_2$ (via $\text{CaPO}_3\text{F}\cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}, \Delta G_f^\circ = -2221.29\text{ kJ/mol}$) to form $50\text{–}60\text{ nm}$ **fluorapatite ($\text{Ca}_5(\text{PO}_4)_3\text{F}$, half-life $59.7\text{ d}$)**, $\sim 50\text{ nm}$ **$\text{CaF}_2$ ($40.8\text{ d}$, $7.55\%$ contraction)**, **hydroxyapatite ($\text{Ca}_5(\text{PO}_4)_3\text{OH}, -152.71\text{ kJ/mol}$)**, and **canaphite ($\text{Na}_2\text{CaP}_2\text{O}_7 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O}, -4.29\text{ kJ/mol}$)** ($63.0\%$ amorphous/$37.0\%$ crystalline; recovering carbonated pH by **$98.85\%$ in CEM I** and **$79.82\%$ in CEM III/B**; Bastidas et al., 2021, Eqs. 10–18):
  $$6\text{Ca(OH)}_2\text{(s)} + 3\text{PO}_3\text{F}^{2-}\text{(aq)} + 6\text{Na}^+\text{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Ca}_5(\text{PO}_4)_3\text{F(s)} + \text{CaF}_2\text{(s)} + 6\text{OH}^-\text{(aq)} + 6\text{Na}^+\text{(aq)} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$$
  $$5\text{Ca(OH)}_2\text{(s)} + 3\text{PO}_4^{3-}\text{(aq)} + 9\text{Na}^+\text{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Ca}_5(\text{PO}_4)_3\text{OH(s)} + 9\text{OH}^-\text{(aq)} + 9\text{Na}^+\text{(aq)}$$
  In thin-mortar $\text{Cl}^-$ tests, efficiency followed **$\text{MFP} > \text{DHP} > \text{TSP}$** ($D_{\text{eff}} = 1.8 \times 10^{-8} > 6.7 \times 10^{-9} > 5.0 \times 10^{-9}\text{ cm}^2/\text{s}$; MCI depths **$1,114\text{–}1,400\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ for MFP**, **$380\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ DHP**, **$126\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ TSP**; DHP best in $\text{Cl}^-$-free ACI at $\sim 1\text{ wt\%}$, while MFP retards hydration). Crucially, portlandite/gel precipitation **immobilizes $\text{PO}_3\text{F}^{2-}$**, so surface-applied MFP **protects only for concrete cover $\le 1\text{ cm}$** (Bastidas et al., 2021), with Ngala et al. (2003) and Douche-Portanguen et al. (2005) detecting no $\text{PO}_3\text{F}^{2-}$ reaching normal rebar depth.
* **Oilfield Brines and Surface Conversion Windows:** In $265\,^\circ\text{F}$ ($129\,^\circ\text{C}, 150\text{ psi}$, 7-d) non-zinc brines, phosphate/hybrid `Chemicals A–D` ($2,500\text{–}10,000\text{ ppm}$) cut P110 corrosion from $>25\text{ mpy}$ ($230\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ pits) to $<3.4\text{–}4\text{ mpy}$ (`A`, $10.8\text{–}11.6\text{ PPG CaCl}_2$), $<0.05\text{ mpy}$ (`B`, $12.5\text{ PPG Ca(NO}_3)_2$), $<0.6\text{–}2\text{ mpy}$ with $<5\text{ }\mu\text{m}$ pits (`C`, $13.5\text{ PPG CaCl}_2/\text{Ca(NO}_3)_2$), and $<1\text{ mpy}$ (`D`; Obeyesekere et al., 2015), alongside $1:1$ phosphonate–gluconate blends ($2,000\text{–}5,000\text{ ppm}$ at $50\,^\circ\text{C}$; Zaid et al., 2018). Conversion bath windows (Kiesow, 2016; Tamilselvi et al., 2015): (1) **$\text{Zn}$ phosphating** ($\text{pH } 2.2\text{–}3.2, 50\text{–}85\,^\circ\text{C}$, **$1.5\text{–}6.5\text{ g/m}^2$**); (2) **$\text{Fe}$ phosphating** ($\text{pH } 4.0\text{–}6.0, 25\text{–}65\,^\circ\text{C}$, **$0.2\text{–}0.8\text{ g/m}^2$**); and (3) **$\text{Mn}$ phosphating** ($\text{pH } 2.2\text{–}2.4, 90\text{–}95\,^\circ\text{C}$, **$10\text{–}40\text{ g/m}^2$**). In primers, $5\%\text{ Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ beat $7\%$ $\text{Zn}$-free rivals at $336\text{ h}$ salt spray, and $4\%$ organic diacid $+ 18.5\%$ Ca-exchanged silica beat $\text{SrCrO}_4$ on HDG/Al-Zn (Gichuhi, 2021). In $1\text{ N H}_2\text{SO}_4$ on `17Kh1T` SS, **$6\text{ g/L H}_3\text{PMo}_{12}\text{O}_{40}\cdot 15\text{H}_2\text{O}$** shifts $E_{\text{st}}$ $+957\text{ mV}$ ($-275 \rightarrow +682\text{ mV}$) and cuts $I_{\text{pas}}$ from $9 \rightarrow 4\text{ mA/cm}^2$, beating $9\text{ g/L Na}_2\text{MoO}_4$ ($+470\text{ mV}$) and $12\text{ g/L H}_3\text{PW}_{12}\text{O}_{40}$ ($+20\text{ mV}$) by packing $25$ Keggin anions ($1.1\text{ nm}$) per $10\times 10\times 1\text{ nm}^3$ (Kazansky et al., 2013).

## 4. Silicate-Based Inhibitors

Silicate inhibitors ($\text{M}_2\text{O} \cdot n\text{SiO}_2$, $\text{M} = \text{Na}^+, \text{K}^+, \text{Li}^+, n = 1.0\text{–}4.0$ modulus, plus $\text{CaSiO}_3, \text{SrSiO}_3, \text{Zn}_2\text{SiO}_4$, phosphosilicates, and borosilicates) are non-toxic, phosphorus-free, $\text{O}_2$-requiring mixed/precipitation, alkalizing, and adsorption agents (Veolia, Ch. 24; US EPA, 2016, 2022; Yuan et al., 2010; Dumpawar, 2015). Comparisons must specify whether $\text{SiO}_2:\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ is molar or mass-based and whether dose is product, $\text{Si}$, or $\text{SiO}_2$ (US EPA, 2022).

### 4.1 Basic Working Principle: Reconciling Four Domain-Specific Models

1. **Alkalization vs. Specific Film Formation:** Silicates hydrate and condense ($\text{SiO}_3^{2-} + 2\text{H}^+ + (n-1)\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{SiO}_2\cdot n\text{H}_2\text{O(s)}$ and $\equiv\text{Si–OH} + \text{HO–Si}\equiv \rightarrow \equiv\text{Si–O–Si}\equiv + \text{H}_2\text{O}$), but in unbuffered perchlorate ($\text{pH } 9.6\text{–}11.6$), Armstrong et al. (1994) proved iron inhibition was driven by **alkalization** rather than a specific silicate film. Likewise, in ASTM C692 tests on stressed Type 304 SS under insulation leachates, **higher-sodium silicates ($\text{Na}_4\text{SiO}_4, \text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$ vs. $\text{Na}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_5$ and $\text{K}$-silicate)** best inhibited $\text{Cl}^-/\text{F}^-$ external stress-corrosion cracking (ESCC; Whorlow & Hutto, NUREG/CR-6539, 1997).
2. **Slow Colloidal Adsorption and Mixed Precipitation in Water:** Veolia (Ch. 24) notes the aqueous mechanism is not fully established: silicates are non-oxidizing, require $\text{O}_2$, form no visible precipitate, and inhibit via **slow adsorption/mixed precipitation** (Rajesh KS, 2021), requiring **2–3 weeks for full protection** as $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3 + (x+1)\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightleftharpoons 2\text{Na}^+ + 2\text{OH}^- + \text{SiO}_2\cdot x\text{H}_2\text{O}$. Negatively charged polysilicates/colloids migrate electrophoretically to positively charged anodic $\text{Fe}^{2+}/\text{FeOOH}$ sites to form inner ferrosilicates ($\text{FeSiO}_3/\text{Fe}_2(\text{SiO}_3)_3$), while cathodic $\text{Ca}^{2+}/\text{Mg}^{2+}$ and high pH coagulate an outer **$10\text{–}100\text{ nm}$ self-limiting amorphous silica-gel / $\text{CaSiO}_3 \cdot n\text{SiO}_2 \cdot m\text{H}_2\text{O}$ barrier** (Dean et al., 1981; Timofeeva, 2017; Dariva & Galio, 2014).
3. **Cross-Linked $\text{Si–O–Si / Si–O–Zn}$ Conversion Network:** On HDG steel, silicic acid condenses onto $\text{Zn–OH}$ into a chemically bonded $\text{Zn}$-silicate + $\text{ZnO/Zn(OH)}_2$ + $\text{SiO}_2$ network blocking both $\text{Zn} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+} + 2e^-$ and $\text{O}_2$ reduction (Yuan et al., 2010). Polymerization scales with modulus above $\approx 2.0$, peaking at an **optimum $\text{SiO}_2:\text{Na}_2\text{O}$ molar ratio of $\approx 3.50$** ($I_{\text{corr}} = 0.34\text{ }\mu\text{A/cm}^2$, $R_p = 20.97\text{ k}\Omega\cdot\text{cm}^2$, **$5\%$ corroded area after 4 salt-spray cycles** vs. $80\%$ after 1 cycle untreated) and declining at $4.00$ due to over-polymerization (while low ratios dissolve $\text{Zn(OH)}_2$ via excess alkalinity; Yuan et al., 2010).
4. **Saponification and Aliovalent Lattice Distortion in Solid Pigments:** In oleoresinous coatings, $\text{Ca/Sr/Zn/Ba}$ phosphosilicates and borosilicates form fatty-acid soaps that degrade into inhibitive anions and raise interfacial pH (Mayne in USBR, 2017). In **$\text{Ce}_x\text{Ca}_{1-x}\text{SiO}_3$ ($x = 0.05\text{–}0.10$)** and **co-blended $\text{CaSiO}_3\text{–}5\text{ to }15\text{ mol\% Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$**, $\text{Ce}^{3+}/\text{Ca}^{2+}$ and $\text{P}^{5+}/\text{Si}^{4+}$ substitution plus $10\text{ h}$ ball-milling and $300\,^\circ\text{C}$ heating of triclinic $\text{CaSiO}_3$ (PDF 42-0547; buffering electrolyte to $\text{pH } 9.5$) distort the $(04\bar{1})/(330)$ planes ($2\theta = 30^\circ\text{–}35^\circ$) and broaden low-energy FTIR **$\text{Si–O–Si}$ bending vibrations at $550\text{–}570\text{ cm}^{-1}$** (leaving $819, 950, 1,080\text{ cm}^{-1}$ stretching intact), driving selective cleavage in $1\text{ wt\% NaCl}$ to release $\text{Ca}^{2+}, \text{SiO}_3^{2-}, \text{PO}_4^{3-}$ (Surendranath, 2011; Dumpawar, 2015).

### 4.2 Specific Compounds and Subcategories

1. **Sodium Silicates:** **$\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$**, **$\text{Na}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_5$**, **$\text{Na}_4\text{SiO}_4$**, and **liquid waterglass / polysilicates ($\text{Na}_2\text{O} \cdot n\text{SiO}_2$, $n \approx 3.50$ optimum)** (Whorlow & Hutto, 1997; Yuan et al., 2010; US EPA, 2022; UFC 3-230-13, 2023).
2. **Potassium, Lithium & Ethyl Silicates / Silicides:** **$\text{K}_2\text{SiO}_3 / \text{K}_2\text{O}\cdot m\text{SiO}_2$**, **$\text{Li}_2\text{SiO}_3$**, **$\text{TEOS}$ ($\text{Si(OC}_2\text{H}_5)_4$)**, **$\text{Zn}_2\text{SiO}_4$**, **$\text{Zn}$ borosilicate**, and **$\text{MoSi}_2$** (Bogan et al., 2004; USBR, 2017; OhioLINK, 2018; Asaad, 2021).
3. **Alkaline-Earth & Modified Silicate Pigments:** **Wollastonite ($\text{CaSiO}_3$)**, **$\text{SrSiO}_3$**, **$\text{Ca/Sr}$ phosphosilicates**, **$\text{Ca}$ borosilicate**, **$\text{Ca}^{2+}\text{-SiO}_2$**, **$\text{CaSiO}_3\text{–}10\text{ mol\% Ca}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ (`CS/CP 10%`)**, and **$\text{Ce}_x\text{Ca}_{1-x}\text{SiO}_3$** (Surendranath, 2011; Hussain & Lyon, 2013; Dumpawar, 2015; USBR, 2017).

### 4.3 Typical Application Industries, Operating Windows, and Limitations

* **Cooling Water, Engine Jackets, and Potable Water:** Silicates protect **soft makeup water** ($\text{TH} < 5\text{–}10\text{ ppm}$ at $10\text{–}15$ COC) where phosphates lack $\text{Ca}^{2+}$ via a **$125\text{–}175\text{ ppm}$ soft-water package** ($25\text{ wt\%}$ Na-silicate, $5\text{ wt\% TKPP}$, $10\text{ wt\% NaOH}$, $6\text{ wt\% EDTA}$, $5\text{ wt\% PAA}$, $4\text{ wt\% MA/EA/VA}$, $3\text{ wt\% TTA}$; Chen & Yang, 2019, Table 12), and protect closed radiator/diesel-engine jackets via **polysilicate–azole** or **nitrite–silicate–borate** ($30\text{ wt\% NaNO}_2 + 8\text{ wt\% Na}_2\text{SiO}_3 + 6\text{ wt\%}$ borax $+ 15\text{ wt\% NaOH} + 3\text{ wt\% PAA} + 2\text{ wt\% TTA}$ at $350\text{–}700\text{ ppm}$; Table 13; UFC 3-230-13, 2023, Table 6-2) or silicate + gluconate + polyphosphate (US Patent 3,711,246). In potable water ($4\text{–}25\text{ mg/L as SiO}_2$; US EPA, 2016, 2022) and domestic softeners, silicates control "red water" and $\text{Pb/Cu}$, *except* that in circumneutral, low-alkalinity water, **elevated sodium silicate disperses and mobilizes colloidal $\text{Pb/Fe}$ scales**, increasing metal release despite surface $\text{Si}$ deposition (Li et al., 2020/2021).
* **Surface Treatments, Insulation Leachates, and Concrete:** Silicates seal $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ pores, inhibit $\text{Al}$ in $\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4$ pickling (Chen & Yang, 2019), and give $\approx 90\%$ efficiency on $\text{Zn}$ as $\text{Na}_2\text{Si}_2\text{O}_5$ (Yuan et al., 2010), whereas **concentrated alkaline $\text{Na}$-silicate solutions strongly corrode $\text{Al}$** vs. steel by dissolving $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ (Wu et al., 2020). In $1\text{ wt\% NaCl}$ ($1\text{ g/L}$ extract), ball-milling $\text{CaSiO}_3$ raised cold-rolled steel (CRS) $R_p$ from $1.320\text{ k}\Omega$ (BET $98.74\text{ m}^2/\text{g}$) to $4.731\text{ k}\Omega$ ($3.49\text{ m}^2/\text{g}$) and $44.12\text{ k}\Omega$ on AA2024 with $10\text{ mol\% PO}_4$ (Surendranath, 2011), while **`CS/CP 10%` matched $\text{SrCrO}_4$** at $R_p \approx 58,000\text{–}72,000\text{ }\Omega$ ($I_{\text{corr}} < 450\text{ nA/cm}^2$) on CRS and $R_p \approx 120,000\text{ }\Omega$ ($I_{\text{corr}} < 300\text{ nA/cm}^2$) on HDG ($15\text{ mol\%}$ destabilized at high anodic overpotential; Dumpawar, 2015). In concrete, $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3/\text{Li}_2\text{SiO}_3$ form pore-blocking $\text{C-S-H}$ gel (Asaad, 2021).
* **Limitations:** $2\text{–}3\text{ week}$ lag; floc precipitation at $\text{pH} < 6.0$ and depolymerization at $\text{pH} > 10.5\text{–}11.0$; incomplete anodic pitting immunity (`Si200`; Boers, 2018); and **volatile silica steam carryover in high-pressure boilers**, depositing on turbine blades (IAPWS TGD5-13, 2013, §6.12).

# Part Two: Key Chemical Reactions

All five reaction systems below conserve atoms and charge, presenting the **balanced core molecular/ionic equation**, **coupled half-reactions/equilibria**, **process variants**, and **thermodynamic parameters ($298.15\text{ K}$)**.

## 1. Zinc-Based Metal Inhibitor Reacting with Oxygen and Water

In neutral aerated water, anodic $\text{Zn}$ oxidation and cathodic $\text{O}_2$ reduction couple—or dry-air $\text{ZnO}$ hydrates—to form an insoluble **zinc hydroxide ($\text{Zn(OH)}_2$) protective layer** (Nordic Galvanizers; Galvanizeit; Dariva & Galio, 2014; Gorejová et al., 2021):
* **Core Balanced Molecular Reaction Equation:**
  $$\boxed{2\text{Zn(s)} + \text{O}_2\text{(g/aq)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \longrightarrow 2\text{Zn(OH)}_2\text{(s)}} \quad (\Delta E^\circ = +1.164\text{ V}, \; \Delta G^\circ = -449.2\text{ kJ/mol})$$
* **Electrochemical Half-Cells, Oxide Hydration, and Variants:**
  1. *Half-cells & ionic precipitation:* $\text{Zn(s)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^-$ ($E^\circ = -0.763\text{ V vs. SHE}$) and $\text{O}_2\text{(g/aq)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} + 4e^- \rightarrow 4\text{OH}^-\text{(aq)}$ ($E^\circ = +0.401\text{ V vs. SHE}$), followed by:
     $$\text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{OH}^-\text{(aq)} \longrightarrow \text{Zn(OH)}_2\text{(s)}\downarrow \quad (K_{\text{sp}} = 3.0 \times 10^{-17}; \; \text{onset pH} = 7.24\text{ at } 1.0\text{ mM }\text{Zn}^{2+})$$
     *(For dissolved $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ salts like $\text{ZnSO}_4/\text{ZnCl}_2$, only this ionic precipitation with cathodic $\text{OH}^-$ occurs; Yesu Thangam et al., 2009.)*
  2. *Two-stage dry oxidation & reversible hydration:* $2\text{Zn(s)} + \text{O}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow 2\text{ZnO(s)}$ and $\text{ZnO(s)} + \text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Zn(OH)}_2\text{(s)}$.
  3. *Deaerated, atmospheric, and concrete variants:* Deaerated water gives $\text{Zn(s)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightarrow \text{Zn(OH)}_2\text{(s)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}\uparrow$; atmospheric $\text{CO}_2$ forms hydrozincite patina ($\text{Zn}_5(\text{CO}_3)_2(\text{OH})_6\text{(s)}$; Saylor General Chemistry); and alkaline concrete ($\text{pH } 12.0\text{–}13.3$) forms calcium hydroxyzincate ($\text{Ca[Zn(OH)}_3]_2 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O(s)}$; Timofeeva, 2017).

## 2. Aluminum-Based Metal Inhibitor Forming an Aluminum Hydroxide Protective Layer

Aluminum reacts in oxygenated water or via direct water reduction (deaerated/hydrolytic $\text{H}_2$ evolution) to form **aluminum hydroxide ($\text{Al(OH)}_3$, bayerite/gibbsite)** (Petrovic & Thomas, 2008 [U.S. DOE]; Howell, 1999/2000; Bunker et al., 2002):
* **Core Balanced Molecular Reaction Equations:**
  * *Aerated Aqueous Route:*
    $$\boxed{4\text{Al(s)} + 3\text{O}_2\text{(g/aq)} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \longrightarrow 4\text{Al(OH)}_3\text{(s)}} \quad (\Delta E^\circ = +2.063\text{ V}, \; \Delta G^\circ = -2388.6\text{ kJ/mol})$$
  * *Direct Water-Reduction Route (Verbatim U.S. DOE Equation, Petrovic & Thomas, 2008):*
    $$\boxed{2\text{Al(s)} + 6\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \longrightarrow 2\text{Al(OH)}_3\text{(s)} + 3\text{H}_2\text{(g)}\uparrow} \quad \left(\text{or } \frac{2}{3}\text{Al} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} = \frac{2}{3}\text{Al(OH)}_3 + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}\right)$$
* **Half-Cells, Amphoteric Dissolution, and Temperature/Passivity Caveats:**
  1. *Half-cells & precipitation:* $\text{Al(s)} \rightarrow \text{Al}^{3+}\text{(aq)} + 3e^-$ ($E^\circ = -1.662\text{ V vs. SHE}$) coupled with $\text{O}_2$ or $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ reduction, precipitating $\text{Al}^{3+}\text{(aq)} + 3\text{OH}^-\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Al(OH)}_3\text{(s)}\downarrow$ ($K_{\text{sp}} = 1.0 \times 10^{-33}$; onset $\text{pH} = 4.00$ at $1.0\text{ mM}$), which dissolves amphoterically at $\text{pH} > 8.5\text{–}9.0$ via $\text{Al(OH)}_3\text{(s)} + \text{OH}^-\text{(aq)} \rightarrow [\text{Al(OH)}_4]^-\text{(aq)}$ (Howell, 1999/2000).
  2. *Thermal windows & duplex film:* Aluminum–water reactions are exothermic ($\approx -280\text{ kJ/mol H}_2$ at $100\,^\circ\text{C}$), forming **bayerite ($\text{Al(OH)}_3$) at $<280\,^\circ\text{C}$**, **boehmite ($\text{AlO(OH)}$) at $280\text{–}480\,^\circ\text{C}$**, and **$\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3$ at $>480\,^\circ\text{C}$** ($2\text{Al(OH)}_3 \rightarrow 2\text{AlOOH} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow \text{Al}_2\text{O}_3 + 3\text{H}_2\text{O}$; Petrovic & Thomas, 2008). In water, an inner coherent $\text{Al}_2\text{O}_3/\gamma\text{-AlOOH}$ layer slows self-corrosion, requiring $\text{In/Ga/Sn}$ activation on sacrificial anodes (Howell, 1999/2000).

## 3. Magnesium Acting as a Sacrificial Anode to Protect Steel

Because $\text{Mg}$ ($E^\circ = -2.372\text{ V vs. SHE}$) is **$1.925\text{ V}$ more negative than $\text{Fe}$ ($-0.447\text{ V}$)**, $\text{Mg}$ oxidizes sacrificially and supplies electrons through the metallic connection to polarize the steel cathode, suppressing $\text{Fe}$ dissolution while driving $\text{O}_2/\text{H}_2\text{O}$ reduction on steel and precipitating brucite ($\text{Mg(OH)}_2$) (US Army TM 5-811-7, 1985; Pathak et al., 2012; Song & Atrens, 2003):
* **Essential Sacrificial Anode Oxidation Half-Reaction (at the $\text{Mg}$ Anode):**
  $$\boxed{\text{Mg(s)} \longrightarrow \text{Mg}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2e^-} \quad (E^\circ = -2.372\text{ V vs. SHE})$$
* **Coupled Galvanic Redox Displacement Equation (Suppressing/Reversing $\text{Fe}^{2+}$ Dissolution):**
  $$\boxed{\text{Mg(s)} + \text{Fe}^{2+}\text{(aq)} \longrightarrow \text{Mg}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Fe(s)}} \quad (\Delta E^\circ = +1.925\text{ V}, \; \Delta G^\circ = -371.5\text{ kJ/mol})$$
* **Overall Aerated Aqueous Sacrificial Cell Reaction (Forming Brucite $\text{Mg(OH)}_2$):**
  $$\boxed{2\text{Mg(s)} + \text{O}_2\text{(g/aq)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \longrightarrow 2\text{Mg(OH)}_2\text{(s)}}$$
* **Protected Steel Cathodic Half-Reactions & Deaerated/NDE Balance:** On the steel cathode surface, $\text{O}_2 + 2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 4e^- \rightarrow 4\text{OH}^-$ (aerated), $2\text{H}_2\text{O} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{H}_2\uparrow + 2\text{OH}^-$ (deaerated/overprotection), and $\text{Fe}^{2+} + 2e^- \rightarrow \text{Fe(s)}$, while $\text{Mg}^{2+} + 2\text{OH}^- \rightarrow \text{Mg(OH)}_2\text{(s)}\downarrow$ ($K_{\text{sp}} = 5.6 \times 10^{-12}$, onset $\text{pH} = 9.87$ at $1.0\text{ mM}$) and $\text{Mg(s)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightarrow \text{Mg(OH)}_2\text{(s)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}\uparrow$ (deaerated/NDE self-corrosion). *(Metallic $\text{Fe}$ is absent from the net cell stoichiometry because the protected steel acts as the inert cathode surface; US Army TM 5-811-7, 1985.)*

## 4. Zinc Phosphate Inhibitor Forming Insoluble Zinc Phosphate on the Metal Surface

Insoluble **tertiary zinc phosphate ($\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ / hopeite $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$)** and **phosphophyllite ($\text{Zn}_2\text{Fe(PO}_4)_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O}$)** form via direct ionic precipitation in water/coatings or acid-consuming hydrolytic equilibrium shift in phosphating baths ($\text{pH } 2.2\text{–}3.2, 50\text{–}85\,^\circ\text{C}$; Simescu & Idrissi, 2008; Tamilselvi et al., 2015; Kiesow, 2016):
* **Pathway A — Direct Ionic Precipitation (Water Treatment & Pigment Leaching):**
  $$\boxed{3\text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{PO}_4^{3-}\text{(aq)} \longrightarrow \text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(s)}\downarrow} \quad (K_{\text{sp}} = 9.0 \times 10^{-33})$$
  $$3\text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{PO}_4^{3-}\text{(aq)} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \longrightarrow \text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O(s)}\downarrow \quad (\text{hopeite})$$
  $$3\text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{HPO}_4^{2-}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{OH}^-\text{(aq)} \longrightarrow \text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(s)}\downarrow + 2\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \quad (\text{alkaline cathodes})$$
* **Pathway B — Phosphating Conversion Bath (from Soluble Primary $\text{Zn(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2$ / $\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-$):**
  1. *Micro-anodic steel dissolution:* $\text{Fe(s)} + 2\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Fe(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}\uparrow$
  2. *Two-step hydrolytic dissociation:* $\text{Zn(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{ZnHPO}_4\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)}$ and $3\text{ZnHPO}_4\text{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(s)}\downarrow + \text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)}$
  3. *Overall Bath Hydrolysis and Explicit Proton-Balance Crystallization Equations:*
     $$\boxed{3\text{Zn(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(s)}\downarrow + 4\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)}}$$
     $$\boxed{3\text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-\text{(aq)} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \longrightarrow \text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O(s)}\downarrow + 4\text{H}^+\text{(aq)}} \quad (\text{hopeite})$$
     $$\boxed{2\text{Zn}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + \text{Fe}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-\text{(aq)} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \longrightarrow \text{Zn}_2\text{Fe(PO}_4)_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O(s)}\downarrow + 4\text{H}^+\text{(aq)}} \quad (\text{phosphophyllite})$$
  4. *Coupled Molecular Equations on Steel/Zinc:* $\text{Fe(s)} + 3\text{Zn(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(s)}\downarrow + \text{Fe(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(aq)} + 2\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}\uparrow$; $\text{Fe(s)} + 2\text{Zn(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(aq)} + 4\text{H}_2\text{O(l)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}_2\text{Fe(PO}_4)_2 \cdot 4\text{H}_2\text{O(s)}\downarrow + 2\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)} + \text{H}_2\text{(g)}\uparrow$; and $3\text{Zn(s)} + 2\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(s)}\downarrow + 3\text{H}_2\text{(g)}\uparrow$.

## 5. Overall Reaction Equation for Iron Phosphate Inhibitor Forming a Protective Layer

Because iron has two oxidation states, balanced equations must distinguish **iron(III) (ferric) phosphate ($\text{FePO}_4$)** from **iron(II) (ferrous) phosphate ($\text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$)** (Bastidas et al., 2021; Mohamed et al., 2022; Kiesow, 2016):
* **Pathway A — Iron(III) Route (Aerated / Oxidative Passivation Forming $\text{FePO}_4$):**
  1. *Overall Molecular Equation:*
     $$\boxed{4\text{Fe(s)} + 4\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)} + 3\text{O}_2\text{(g/aq)} \longrightarrow 4\text{FePO}_4\text{(s)}\downarrow + 6\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}}$$
  2. *Industrial Alkali-Metal Primary Phosphate Bath ($\text{pH } 4\text{–}5.5$, Forming Amorphous $\text{FePO}_4 / \gamma\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3$):*
     $$4\text{Fe(s)} + 4\text{NaH}_2\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)} + 3\text{O}_2\text{(g)} \longrightarrow 2\text{FePO}_4\text{(s)}\downarrow + \gamma\text{-Fe}_2\text{O}_3\text{(s)}\downarrow + 2\text{Na}_2\text{HPO}_4\text{(aq)} + 3\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$$
  3. *From Ferric Hydroxide:* $\text{Fe(OH)}_3\text{(s)} + \text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{FePO}_4\text{(s)}\downarrow + 3\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$.
* **Pathway B — Iron(II) Route (Phosphoric Acid & Aerated Primary Phosphate Baths Forming $\text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$):**
  1. *Non-oxidizing acid bath:* Micro-anodic dissolution $3\text{Fe(s)} + 6\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)} \rightarrow 3\text{Fe(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(aq)} + 3\text{H}_2\text{(g)}\uparrow$ plus hydrolysis $3\text{Fe(H}_2\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(s)}\downarrow + 4\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)}$ gives uncanceled $3\text{Fe(s)} + 6\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(s)}\downarrow + 4\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)} + 3\text{H}_2\text{(g)}\uparrow$, reducing to the **Net Canceled Overall Equation**:
     $$\boxed{3\text{Fe(s)} + 2\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4\text{(aq)} \longrightarrow \text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(s)}\downarrow + 3\text{H}_2\text{(g)}\uparrow}$$
  2. *Aerated primary phosphate bath ($\text{pH } 4\text{–}6$; Kiesow, 2016):* $3\text{Fe(s)} + 2\text{H}^+\text{(aq)} + 2\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^-\text{(aq)} + \frac{3}{2}\text{O}_2\text{(g)} \rightarrow \text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(s)}\downarrow + 3\text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$ (or $6\text{Fe} + 4\text{H}^+ + 4\text{H}_2\text{PO}_4^- + 3\text{O}_2 \rightarrow 2\text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\downarrow + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
* **Pathway C — Net Ionic Precipitation Equations (Bastidas et al., 2021; Mohamed et al., 2022):**
  $$\boxed{3\text{Fe}^{2+}\text{(aq)} + 2\text{PO}_4^{3-}\text{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{Fe}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2\text{(s)}\downarrow} \quad (\Delta G_f^\circ = -2444.80\text{ kJ/mol}, \; pK_{\text{sp}} = 32\text{ to }36; \; \text{vivianite: }\cdot 8\text{H}_2\text{O})$$
  $$\boxed{\text{Fe}^{3+}\text{(aq)} + \text{PO}_4^{3-}\text{(aq)} \rightleftharpoons \text{FePO}_4\text{(s)}\downarrow} \quad (\Delta G_f^\circ = -1663.98\text{ kJ/mol}, \; pK_{\text{sp}} = 21.89\text{ to }26; \; \text{strengite: }\cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O})$$

## Synthesis of Scientific Controversies, Boundaries, and Engineering Selection Rules

1. **Reconciling Silicate Mechanisms:** Unbuffered perchlorate ($\text{pH } 9.6\text{–}11.6$) inhibition is driven by alkalization (Armstrong et al., 1994); bulk cooling/potable water operates via slow ($2\text{–}3\text{ week}$) colloidal adsorption and mixed precipitation over nascent rust (Veolia, Ch. 24; Rajesh KS, 2021); HDG conversion baths form cross-linked $\text{Si–O–Si}/\text{Si–O–Zn}$ networks peaking at $\text{SiO}_2:\text{Na}_2\text{O} \approx 3.50$ (Yuan et al., 2010); and solid pigments act via fatty-acid soaps and $550\text{–}570\text{ cm}^{-1}$ $\text{Si–O–Si}$ bending distortion (Dumpawar, 2015; USBR, 2017)—bounded by concentrated alkaline attack on $\text{Al}$ (Wu et al., 2020) and colloidal $\text{Pb/Fe}$ mobilization in low-alkalinity potable water (Li et al., 2020/2021).
2. **Phosphate Stature vs. Aerospace Limits & Policy Paradoxes:** Phosphates are premier bulk water/boiler, rebar, and commodity primer inhibitors (Veolia, Ch. 11 & 24; Wan et al., 2017; Bastidas et al., 2021), yet cannot replace $\text{Cr(VI)}$ in aerospace conversion coatings due to narrow $\text{pH } 5\text{–}10$ stability (vs. $\text{pH } 2\text{–}12$ for $\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_3$) and lack of redox self-healing (Gharbi et al., 2018), while SKP shows $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ is the active disbondment inhibitor in $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2$ with $\text{PO}_4^{3-}$ regulating $\text{Zn}^{2+}$ solubility (Griffiths et al., 2022). Meanwhile, Lead and Copper Rule Revisions push utilities toward $1\text{–}3\text{ mg/L PO}_4$ tap dosing just as wastewater eutrophication limits and EU $\text{Zn}_3(\text{PO}_4)_2/\text{ZnO}$ aquatic-toxicity labels push industry toward $\text{P}$- and $\text{Zn}$-free chemistry (US EPA, 2016; Hazen & Sawyer, 2022; Gichuhi, 2021).
3. **Cross-Sector Engineering Verdict:**
   * **Concrete Reinforcement, Closed Loops & Nuclear Coolant:** Admixed **$\text{Ca(NO}_2)_2, \text{Ca(NO}_3)_2, \text{LiNO}_2, \text{NaNO}_2$** and **$\text{Na}_2\text{MoO}_4/\text{Na}_2\text{B}_4\text{O}_7$** prevail because fresh admixing places passivators directly at the rebar ($n(\text{NO}_2^-)/n(\text{Cl}^-) > 0.5\text{–}1.2$), avoiding the portlandite/apatite immobilization of surface-applied $\text{Na}_2\text{PO}_3\text{F}$ (effective only for $\le 1\text{ cm}$ cover; Ngala et al., 2003; Douche-Portanguen et al., 2005; Bastidas et al., 2021), the 2-yr decay of admixed phosphates, and the $20\text{ mm}$ limit of migrating nitrites (Bolzoni et al., 2014; Kim et al., 2015; Song et al., 2019).
   * **Open Cooling Towers & Municipal Water:** **Stabilized/alkaline $\text{Zn}$–phosphate and $\text{ZOP}$** dominate in moderate-to-hard waters ($300\text{–}1,200\text{ ppm CaCO}_3$, $\text{pH } 7.2\text{–}7.8$), flipping to **sodium silicate / polysilicate–azole** in soft water ($\text{TH} < 10\text{ ppm}$), engine jackets, or under $\text{P}$-discharge bans (Guan, 2007; US EPA, 2016; Chen & Yang, 2019; Hazen & Sawyer, 2022; UFC 3-230-13, 2023).
   * **High-Pressure Boilers ($>10.3\text{ MPa} / \ge 1,000\text{ psig}$) & Deep Hot Completion/Production Waters ($129\text{–}204\,^\circ\text{C}$):** Thermal decomposition and carryover rule out acidic $\text{NaH}_2\text{PO}_4/\text{Na}_2\text{HPO}_4$ (maricite $\text{NaFePO}_4$ APC), $\text{Na}_2\text{SiO}_3$ (turbine blade deposits; IAPWS, 2013), $\text{Na}_2\text{SO}_3$ ($\text{SO}_2/\text{H}_2\text{S}$ above $1,000\text{ psig}$), and non-zinc $\text{NaSCN}$ ($300\,^\circ\text{F}$ $\text{H}_2\text{S}$ SCC of $13\text{Cr}/22\text{Cr}$), mandating **equilibrium $\text{Na}_3\text{PO}_4$ (TSP-only, $\text{Na}:\text{PO}_4 \ge 3.0$) + $\text{N}_2\text{H}_4$ over $\text{Fe}_3\text{O}_4$** in boilers (Veolia, Ch. 11; Dooley & Bursik, 2010; IAPWS, 2015) and **brine-soluble phosphate/phosphonate–gluconate blends**, external **$\text{Al–Zn–In}/\text{Zn}$ anodes** (DNV-RP-B401, 2010/2011), or **sequential bisulfite-then-nitrate injection** (monitoring for $\text{NO}_2^-/\text{S}^0$ metabolites; Lahme et al., 2019; Fida et al., 2021) in oilfield systems (Mack et al., 2002; McKennis & Carter, 2005; Bødtker et al., 2008; Gittel et al., 2009; Obeyesekere et al., 2015; Zaid et al., 2018).
