# In-Depth Research Report: Green Bond External Reviewers (GBERs) in China (Through End-2022)

**Historical Scope, Terminology, and Methodological Note**  
This report examines the role, market structure, regulatory regime, and credibility risks of Green Bond External Reviewers (GBERs) in China through **31 December 2022**. Post-2022 publications—such as the China Green Bond Standards Committee’s (CGBSC) July 2023 operational notice, Lianhe Ratings’ March 2023 annual report, and post-2022 Climate Bonds Initiative (CBI) reports—are used strictly for retrospective calendar-year 2022 data or explicitly marked post-window context.

While “GBER” is an umbrella term for independent third-party organizations that evaluate, verify, certify, or rate a green bond’s environmental credentials and fund-tracking mechanisms pre- and post-issuance, it is not a single licensed profession. International usage prefers *external review providers*, whereas Chinese texts collapse all four internationally recognized review types into **第三方评估认证机构** (third-party assessment and certification/verification institutions) or **评估认证** (assessment and certification/verification). Translating every Chinese instance of 评估认证 as formal international “certification” is misleading, as domestic reports frequently blend multiple review activities into one document. Throughout the market-share analysis, two non-interchangeable statistical bases are preserved and reconciled: **amount-based shares (Caliber A)** (CBI/CCDC basis: share of reviewed issuance value across onshore and offshore Chinese deals, all review types combined) and **count-based shares (Caliber B)** (Chinese rating/research houses such as Lianhe Ratings and CGBSC: share of domestic certified bond issues, denoted in *期* or *只*).

## Why External Review Exists: Addressing Information Asymmetry and Greenwashing

Green bonds earmark proceeds for environmentally beneficial projects (e.g., renewable energy, pollution control, clean transport). Because they attract policy incentives, faster regulatory approval, and a yield discount (“greenium”), issuers face financial temptation to engage in **greenwashing** (**洗绿/漂绿**—labeling ordinary financing as green without genuine environmental gains).

Investors generally lack the expertise to verify environmental commitments independently. As Lianhe Equator practitioners Zhao Mengying and Liu Jingyun noted on 2 April 2017, investors “generally lack environmental expertise and find it difficult to accurately judge green credentials,” harboring doubts over “the use and management of proceeds, project categories, and the truthfulness of disclosure” ([Zhao & Liu, greenfinance.org.cn, April 2017](http://www.greenfinance.org.cn/displaynews.php?id=803)). External review acts as the primary safeguard against greenwashing. Chinese official media call these reviewers the market’s “gatekeepers” (**看门人**), a role emphasized when the CGBSC (**绿标委**—the self-regulatory body established on 7 December 2018 under Article 5 of *PBOC/CSRC Announcement [2017] No. 20*, with its secretariat at NAFMII) launched its registration regime in 2021–2022 ([Xinhua, Sept 2022](https://www.news.cn/fortune/2022-09/29/c_1129042110.htm); [China City News, Oct 2022](http://paper.people.com.cn/zgcsbwap/html/2022-10/10/content_25943206.htm)).

## 1. GBER Types and Comparison

### 1.1 Systematic Introduction to the Four Internationally Recognized GBER Types

The International Capital Market Association (ICMA), publisher of the voluntary *Green Bond Principles* (GBP, built on four pillars: use of proceeds, project evaluation and selection, management of proceeds, and reporting), classifies external reviews in its *Guidelines for Green, Social, Sustainability and Sustainability-Linked Bonds External Reviews* ([June 2020](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Regulatory/Green-Bonds/June-2020/External-Review-GuidelinesJune-2020-090620.pdf), [February 2021](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/Guidelines-for-GreenSocialSustainability-and-Sustainability-Linked-Bonds-External-Reviews-February-2021-170221.pdf), [June 2022](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/2022-updates/External-Review-Guidelines_June-2022-280622.pdf)) into four types: **Second-Party Opinion (SPO)**, **Certification**, **Verification**, and **Green Bond Rating/Scoring**. Providers may offer one or multiple services separately or combined. Empirical pricing research by [Dorfleitner, Utz, and Zhang (2021)](https://d-nb.info/1233658204/34) and institutional analysis by [Ehlers and Packer (2017)](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1709h.pdf) at the Bank for International Settlements (BIS), alongside Chinese grading frameworks, show that each type addresses a distinct investor question across a trade-off frontier of standardization, comparability, and information depth.

| Review Type | Core Investor Question | Typical Output | Typical Providers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Second-Party Opinion (SPO)** | Does the proposed framework make environmental sense and align with stated principles? | Detailed narrative opinion, often with a qualitative “shade of green” (*Dark / Medium / Light Green*) | ESG research/analytics firms, climate research bodies, environmental consultancies, and rating agencies (e.g., CICERO, Sustainalytics, Vigeo Eiris, ISS-Oekom [ISS-ESG], DNV GL, S&P Global, Moody’s, Sustainable Fitch globally; Lianhe Equator / 联合赤道, CECEP Consulting / CECEP Hundred / 中节能咨询/中节能衡准, SynTao Green Finance / 商道融绿, Zhongcai Green Financing / 中财绿融, iGreenBank / 绿融投资 domestically) |
| **Certification** | Does the bond/framework satisfy a recognized external standard or label? | Binary certification decision or mark/label from a standard body, backed by a verifier’s conformity statement | Standard-setting schemes (CBI’s Climate Bonds Standard, HKQAA) paired with accredited “Approved Verifiers”: accounting firms (EY, Deloitte, KPMG, PwC), TIC bodies (CQC, CTI Certification, SinoCarbon, Bureau Veritas, DNV GL, SGS, Lloyd’s Register, RINA, HKQAA), ESG agencies (Sustainalytics, Vigeo Eiris, ISS), and domestic consultancies/rating affiliates (SynTao, Zhongcai, Lianhe Equator, CCX/CCXGF, CECEP Hundred, Golden Credit, iGreenBank, CSCI Pengyuan Green Finance, Carbon Care Asia, Raising Clean-tech) |
| **Verification (Assurance)** | Does specified information, conduct, or performance conform to designated criteria? | 2–3 page formal verification/assurance statement (*Reasonable* or *Limited Assurance*, positive/negative compliance opinion) | Accounting/auditing firms (Big Four: EY, Deloitte, KPMG, PwC), assurance specialists, and technical inspection/certification bodies (DNV GL, Bureau Veritas, SGS, CQC, CTC, CTI Certification, HKQAA) |
| **Green Bond Rating / Scoring** | How strong are the assessed green characteristics under a specific methodology? | Numerical score (0–100) or ordinal letter grade (international GB1–GB5 / CGA–CGO; domestic G1–G4 / NG, G-1–G-5, GB-1–GB-4) | Credit rating agencies, environmental data firms, and rating-affiliated GBERs (e.g., Moody’s, S&P Global Ratings, JCR, R&I, RAM Holdings, Trucost globally; China Bond Rating / 中债资信, Lianhe Equator, CCXI / CCX Green Finance / 中诚信, Golden Credit / 东方金诚, Far East Credit Rating / 远东资信, Shanghai Brilliance / 上海新世纪, Shenzhen Pengyuan Green Finance / 鹏元绿融, Dagong Low Carbon / 大公低碳 domestically) |

#### 1. Second-Party Opinion (SPO)
* **Definition:** An independent, predominantly pre-issuance expert judgment by an institution with environmental expertise—independent of the issuer and its framework adviser, or operating with “Chinese Walls” and disclosure—on whether an issuer’s *framework* (project selection, proceeds management, and expected environmental benefits) aligns with relevant principles (e.g., ICMA GBP/GLP). Beyond checking procedural compliance, an SPO evaluates eligible projects’ environmental features, expected benefits, material ESG risks, and the issuer’s broader sustainability strategy ([ICMA 2020](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Regulatory/Green-Bonds/June-2020/External-Review-GuidelinesJune-2020-090620.pdf); [ICMA 2021](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/Guidelines-for-GreenSocialSustainability-and-Sustainability-Linked-Bonds-External-Reviews-February-2021-170221.pdf); [ICMA 2022](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/2022-updates/External-Review-Guidelines_June-2022-280622.pdf)). Leading providers often assign a qualitative “shade of green” (Dark, Medium, Light Green, or Brown) indicating alignment with a long-term low-carbon transition.
* **Typical Providers:** Climate/environmental research institutes, scientific/semi-public bodies, ESG analytics agencies, specialized green consultancies, and rating agencies (internationally: CICERO, Sustainalytics, Vigeo Eiris, ISS-Oekom, DNV GL, S&P Global, Moody’s, Sustainable Fitch; in China: Lianhe Equator, CECEP Consulting / CECEP Hundred, SynTao Green Finance, Beijing Zhongcai Green Financing, iGreenBank) ([CBI 2018](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-2018.pdf); [CBI 2021](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-Report-2021.pdf); [Dorfleitner et al. 2021](https://d-nb.info/1233658204/34)).
* **Main Advantages:**
  * *Temporal coverage of unspent proceeds:* Certifies a forward-looking commitment and project-selection framework prior to issuance rather than historical transactions.
  * *Explanatory breadth, granularity, and pricing impact:* Delivers narrative evaluation of project eligibility, environmental science, governance, issuer strategy, and climate tradeoffs. CICERO’s “shades of green” grades frameworks on a three-step scale ([Ehlers & Packer, BIS 2017](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1709h.pdf)). Across 250 global green bonds, SPOs reduce investor search costs and generate a statistically significant secondary-market greenium of **3 to 5 bps** (3.55 bps overall, $p<0.01$), rising to **5.36 bps** for Dark Green SPOs versus bonds without an SPO ([Dorfleitner et al. 2021](https://d-nb.info/1233658204/34)).
  * *Flexibility and cost:* Directly assesses the scientific rationale of bespoke or transitional decarbonization projects not covered by fixed sector taxonomies, at a lower cost than formal financial assurance or multi-stage certification.
* **Main Disadvantages:**
  * *Methodological customization, subjectivity, and cross-provider incomparability:* Providers emerged organically without a mandatory standard, differing in assumptions, exclusions, depth, and grading vocabularies ([EU Commission SWD(2021) 181](https://www.eu.dk/samling/20211/kommissionsforslag/KOM(2021)0391/forslag/1799527/2428188.pdf)). Investors must evaluate each bond individually, as SPOs disclose little about the exact environmental performance standard applied ([Zhao & Liu 2017](http://www.greenfinance.org.cn/displaynews.php?id=803)). Empirically, CICERO uses *Dark, Medium, Light Green*; Vigeo Eiris uses *Reasonable, Moderate, Weak*; ISS-Oekom uses *Excellent, Good, Medium, Poor*; and Sustainalytics uses *Leader, Outperformer, Average Performer, Underperformer*, while **38.0% of bonds with an SPO** (95 of 196) in [Dorfleitner et al. (2021)](https://d-nb.info/1233658204/34) carried no explicit shade.
  * *Point-in-time nature:* Evaluates the framework at issuance without guaranteeing subsequent allocation or realized impact; CICERO does not monitor ex post changes unless specifically requested ([Ehlers & Packer 2017](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1709h.pdf)).
  * *Lack of formal assurance and independence threats:* Remains an opinion rather than a formal auditing engagement, with self-review risks if the reviewer helped design the framework ([ICMA 2022](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/2022-updates/External-Review-Guidelines_June-2022-280622.pdf)).

#### 2. Certification
* **Definition:** A formal, binary conformity-assessment and labeling mechanism testing a bond or framework against a recognized external green standard with quantitative, sector-specific technical screening criteria, culminating in an official certification mark awarded by the standard-setting body ([ICMA 2021](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/Guidelines-for-GreenSocialSustainability-and-Sustainability-Linked-Bonds-External-Reviews-February-2021-170221.pdf)). Under the flagship Climate Bonds Standard (CBS) of the Climate Bonds Initiative (CBI)—which sets sector thresholds aligned with $1.5^\circ\text{C}$/$2^\circ\text{C}$ Paris Agreement targets, governed at the end-2022 cutoff by **Climate Bonds Standard v3.0 (December 2019)** ([CBS v3.0](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/Climate-Bonds_Climate-Bonds-Standard_V3_Dec-2019.pdf))—certification separates the standard-setting authority (CBI’s Climate Bonds Standard Board) that grants the mark from the CBI-accredited “Approved Verifier” hired by the issuer for pre- and post-issuance conformity audits. Approved Verifiers must demonstrate competence in (1) capital-markets bond issuance/proceeds management, (2) technical performance of low-carbon projects under CBS criteria, and (3) assurance services under international standards ([CBI, Become an Approved Verifier](https://www.climatebonds.net/expertise/standard-sector-criteria-certification/certification/become-approved-verifier)). In Hong Kong SAR, the Hong Kong Quality Assurance Agency (HKQAA) launched an analogous Green Finance Certification Scheme in March 2018.
* **Typical Providers:** Standard-setting bodies (CBI, HKQAA) paired with accredited Approved Verifiers active in China’s onshore/offshore markets through end-2022: accounting firms (EY China, Deloitte, KPMG, PwC), TIC bodies (CQC, CTI Certification, SinoCarbon, Bureau Veritas, DNV GL, SGS, Lloyd’s Register, RINA, HKQAA), ESG agencies (Sustainalytics, Vigeo Eiris, ISS), and domestic consultancies/rating affiliates (SynTao, Zhongcai, Lianhe Equator, CCX/CCXGF, CECEP Hundred, Golden Credit, iGreenBank, CSCI Pengyuan Green Finance, Carbon Care Asia, Raising Clean-tech) ([CBI 2017](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/drupal-files/files/China_Annual_Report_2017_English.pdf); [CBI 2018](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-2018.pdf); [CBI Nov 2019 Verifiers](https://www.climatebonds.net/news-events/blog/new-approved-verifiers-climate-bonds-standard-access-certification-services-grows-green-bond-loan-issuers); [CBI 2020](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-2019-Research-Report_2025-02-17-163051_qijt.pdf); [SGS 2020](https://www.sgs.com/en-ch/news/2020/06/sgs-gains-approved-verifier-status-for-climate-bonds-certification); [CTI 2021](https://www.cti-cert.com/en/service/6604/6894.html)).
* **Main Advantages:**
  * *Science-based eligibility benchmark and highest cross-border comparability:* Published sector criteria (solar, wind, low-carbon buildings, transport, water) and supervised verifiers eliminate eligibility ambiguity, serving as a low-cost screening tool without requiring independent due diligence on local taxonomies ([Fangda Partners 2022](https://www.fangdalaw.com/content/details143_8811.html)). Described by CBI and BIS as “the only Paris Agreement aligned standard available in the market” ([Ehlers & Packer 2017](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1709h.pdf)), CBS certification was sought by Chinese offshore issuers to prove environmental integrity to international investors ([CBI 2018](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-2018.pdf)).
  * *Mandatory two-stage governance:* After pre-issuance certification, issuers must submit a **post-issuance assurance report** confirming actual proceeds allocation to retain certified status ([CBI 2018](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-2018.pdf); [CBI Nov 2019](https://www.climatebonds.net/news-events/blog/new-approved-verifiers-climate-bonds-standard-access-certification-services-grows-green-bond-loan-issuers)), alongside annual allocation/eligibility reporting required under [CBS v3.0](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/Climate-Bonds_Climate-Bonds-Standard_V3_Dec-2019.pdf).
* **Main Disadvantages:**
  * *Binary signal lacking relative granularity:* Certifies only whether a bond meets a threshold floor (“a bond is either green or not” — [Ehlers & Packer 2017](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1709h.pdf); [Zhao & Liu 2017](http://www.greenfinance.org.cn/displaynews.php?id=803)), conveying little about relative environmental ambition or financial additionality.
  * *Coverage, monitoring, and pricing limits:* Covers only sectors with published CBS criteria. Historically, certification did not mandate ongoing monitoring ([Ehlers & Packer 2017](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1709h.pdf)), and the certificate mark alone is uninformative without supporting reports. While onshore Chinese verified/certified bonds (2016–2018) priced ~40–60 bps tighter than unverified ones ([Deng, Tang & Zhang 2020](https://www.lse.ac.uk/economics/Assets/Documents/job-market-2025-26/JM-Zhiyao-Deng-Greenness.pdf)), global secondary-market regressions by [Dorfleitner et al. (2021)](https://d-nb.info/1233658204/34) found no statistically significant marginal yield premium specifically for CBI certification (-2.57 bps, $p>0.10$) once SPOs and verifications are controlled for.
  * *Administrative, speed, and financial cost:* Requires standard-body screening plus separate pre- and post-issuance verifier engagements ([Chinakec 2022](https://www.chinakec.com/prod_view-1092.html)). International benchmarks place CBS certification costs from ~USD 15,000 and HKQAA certification at ~HKD 110,000, versus USD 10,000–100,000 for international SPO/assurance engagements (though no universal fee ranking across all four types exists inside China).

#### 3. Verification (Assurance)
* **Definition:** An independent check of proceeds use and compliance against a defined standard, encompassing (1) *Assurance* (第三方鉴证或核查), where audit firms execute predefined procedures against internal or external criteria (e.g., ICMA GBP/GLP, domestic catalogues, internal fund-tracking controls) to issue a positive or negative compliance opinion; and (2) *Verification for certification*, where a CBS Approved Verifier confirms CBS alignment ([ICMA 2021](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/Guidelines-for-GreenSocialSustainability-and-Sustainability-Linked-Bonds-External-Reviews-February-2021-170221.pdf); [ICMA 2022](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/2022-updates/External-Review-Guidelines_June-2022-280622.pdf)). International audit firm verifications are governed by formal attestation standards—chiefly **ISAE 3000 (Revised)** (effective for reports dated on or after 15 December 2015 — [IAASB 2013](https://www.iaasb.org/news-events/2013-12/iaasb-enhances-standard-assurance-engagements)), AICPA standards, and the IESBA Code of Ethics, alongside ISO 17021 and ISO 14065 ([ICMA 2022](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/2022-updates/External-Review-Guidelines_June-2022-280622.pdf); [CBI Nov 2019](https://www.climatebonds.net/news-events/blog/new-approved-verifiers-climate-bonds-standard-access-certification-services-grows-green-bond-loan-issuers); [Zhao & Liu 2017](http://www.greenfinance.org.cn/displaynews.php?id=803)).
* **Typical Providers:** Big Four accounting firms (EY, Deloitte, PwC, KPMG), assurance specialists, and accredited TIC bodies (DNV GL, Bureau Veritas, SGS, CQC, CTC, CTI Certification, HKQAA, plus CBS Approved Verifiers like Sustainalytics, CCX Green Finance, and SynTao) ([CBI 2017](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/drupal-files/files/China_Annual_Report_2017_English.pdf); [CBI 2021](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-Report-2021.pdf); [CGBSC Sept 2022](https://www.nafmii.org.cn/ztbd/lszqbzwyh/tzgg/202209/P020220921637741774368.pdf)). Accounting firms are “explicitly covered by professional standards and/or regulation” unlike most other providers ([ICMA 2021](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/Guidelines-for-GreenSocialSustainability-and-Sustainability-Linked-Bonds-External-Reviews-February-2021-170221.pdf)).
* **Main Advantages:**
  * *Evidential discipline, procedural objectivity, and pricing credibility:* Imports formal audit working papers and evidence standards to reconcile disbursements to green project ledgers and special accounts. Extends seamlessly to post-issuance tracking and carries a statistically significant secondary-market greenium of **2.46 bps** ($p<0.05$) in [Dorfleitner et al. (2021)](https://d-nb.info/1233658204/34).
  * *Statutory ethics and insurance:* Regulated accounting firms carry statutory independence codes and mandatory professional indemnity insurance, suiting large commercial bank issuers.
* **Main Disadvantages:**
  * *Narrow informational footprint and minimal environmental narrative:* Confirms procedural and paper catalogue compliance rather than engineering quality, lifecycle ecological impact, or transition strategy. Typical verification reports are **only 2 to 3 pages long** ([Dorfleitner et al. 2021](https://d-nb.info/1233658204/34)), comprising a standardized legalistic description of document checks (e.g., a 4-step workflow at Deloitte vs. a 7-step workflow at EY per [Zhihu Industry Research, Aug 2022](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/559612820)) and boilerplate assurance language.
  * *Assurance-level ambiguity:* Under ISAE 3000, “reasonable assurance” (positive opinion) and “limited assurance” (negative opinion: nothing came to attention indicating non-compliance) involve very different testing depths, and non-accountant “verification” may not follow ISAE 3000 at all ([IAASB 2013](https://www.iaasb.org/news-events/2013-12/iaasb-enhances-standard-assurance-engagements)).

#### 4. Green Bond Rating / Scoring
* **Definition:** A quantitative or semi-quantitative assessment grading a bond or framework (alignment with green principles, governance, transparency, and expected impact) via a weighted scorecard to assign an ordinal letter grade or numerical score ([ICMA 2021](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/Guidelines-for-GreenSocialSustainability-and-Sustainability-Linked-Bonds-External-Reviews-February-2021-170221.pdf)). It is **conceptually distinct from a credit rating** (which measures financial default risk), though it carries a practical risk of investor confusion ([Ehlers & Packer 2017](https://www.bis.org/publications/green-bond-finance-and-certification)).
* **International vs. Domestic Chinese Grading Scales:**
  * *International Scales:* Moody’s Green Bond Assessments (GBA, launched March 2016) use a five-grade scale from **GB1 (“Excellent”) to GB5 (“Poor”)** (plus certification marks CGA, CGB, CGC, CGO), weighting **40% Use of Proceeds**, **20% Ongoing Reporting/Disclosure**, **15% Organization**, **15% Management of Proceeds**, and **10% Disclosure on Use of Proceeds** (matching Trucost’s GB1–GB5 weights). S&P Global Ratings’ Green Evaluations (launched 2017) score **0 to 100**, weighting **60% Mitigation (environmental impact)**, **25% Governance** (19% project selection/impact management + 6% proceeds protection), and **15% Transparency**. Japan Credit Rating Agency (JCR) uses Green 1–5 ([Ehlers & Packer 2017](https://www.bis.org/publications/green-bond-finance-and-certification_1.pdf); [Zhihu Industry Research 2022](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/559612820)).
  * *Domestic Chinese Scales (G1–G4 and NG):* “CCX and China Bond Rating were the pioneers in developing green bond rating schemes in China” ([CBI 2018](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-2018.pdf)), encouraged by Article 29 of *PBOC & CSRC Announcement [2017] No. 20*. On **13 December 2016**, China Bond Rating (中债资信, founded August 2010 by NAFMII as China’s first investor-pays CRA) launched the first domestic methodology mapped to the *Green Bond Endorsed Project Catalogue* ([HRLOO, Dec 2016](https://m.hrloo.com/rz/14073994.html); [China Bond Rating / Sina Finance Filing 2023](http://file.finance.sina.com.cn/211.154.219.97:9494/MRGG/BOND/2023/2023-8/2023-08-11/19083097.PDF)). It scores four dimensions on a 4.0 scale—(1) Use and Management of Proceeds, (2) Industrial Policy, (3) Information Disclosure and Reporting, and (4) Governance and Systems—combining the project benefit score with a **sector-specific green grade cap** (preventing transitional projects from exceeding their sector ceiling) to assign five grades:
    * **G1 — Dark Green (深绿):** Extremely low environmental risk; very significant environmental benefits.
    * **G2 — Green (绿):** Very low environmental risk; obvious environmental benefits.
    * **G3 — Moderately Green (较绿):** Low environmental risk; relatively obvious environmental benefits (e.g., 3.22/4.0 composite score).
    * **G4 — Light Green (浅绿):** Relatively low environmental risk; certain environmental benefits.
    * **NG — Non-Green (非绿):** Severe environmental pollution; fails green benefit requirements.
  * Lianhe Equator (联合赤道) established China’s first combined “Entity + Bond” (*主体+债项*) methodology (`LEIS0002-2017`, updated `2019` and `2021`), grading bonds on **G1 (深绿) to G4 (浅绿) and NG (非绿)** (plus enterprise grades G-AAA, G-AA, G-A), while CCX/CCXGF and Shanghai Brilliance adopted **G-1 to G-5** and **GB-1 to GB-4 / NG** scales.
* **Typical Providers:** International CRAs and data firms (Moody’s, S&P, JCR, R&I, RAM Holdings, Trucost) and domestic CRAs/green subsidiaries (China Bond Rating, Lianhe Equator, CCXI/CCXGF, Golden Credit, Far East Credit Rating, Shanghai Brilliance, Shenzhen Pengyuan Green Finance, Dagong Low Carbon) ([CBI 2018](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-2018.pdf); [CBI 2020](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-2019-Research-Report_2025-02-17-163051_qijt.pdf); [CGBSC Sept 2022](https://www.nafmii.org.cn/ztbd/lszqbzwyh/tzgg/202209/P020220921637741774368.pdf)). Standalone green ratings took 3% of China’s 2018 volume (11 deals, including Wuhan Metro’s perpetual bond rated GB1 by Moody’s) and 5% in 2019, though domestic G1–G4 grading was widely embedded inside blended domestic reports.
* **Main Advantages:**
  * *Ordinal differentiation and familiar infrastructure:* Explicit factor weights and multi-tier grades allow ranking by degree of environmental benefit ([Ehlers & Packer 2017](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1709h.pdf)).
  * *Surveillance potential:* Incorporates periodic ex post monitoring and multi-year rating refreshes.
* **Main Disadvantages:**
  * *Proprietary methodologies and cross-agency incomparability:* Scales (Moody’s GB1–GB5, S&P 0–100, CCX G-1–G-5, Brilliance GB-1–GB-4/NG, CBR G1–G4/NG) weight factors differently and are mutually incomparable, while methodology revisions (e.g., `LEIS0002-2017` to `2021`) alter intra-agency comparability over time.
  * *Severe top-grade clustering (grade inflation) and shelf-life limits:* Under the issuer-pays model, as of end-July 2017, **all 26 bond issues** rated under Moody’s GBA held **GB1**, and all 10 S&P-scored issues clustered between 67 and 92 ([Ehlers & Packer 2017](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1709h.pdf)). [Dorfleitner et al. (2021)](https://d-nb.info/1233658204/34) similarly found all 10 rated bonds in their 250-bond sample held top grades (8 Moody’s Excellent, 2 JCR Green 1), rendering green ratings statistically insignificant for secondary-market yield premia (+1.79 bps, $p>0.10$ in hybrid model; +10% level in OLS). In China, G1 (深绿) clustering is similarly pervasive. Moreover, S&P removes its point-in-time Green Evaluation from its website after at most 18 months unless an ex post evaluation is commissioned ([Ehlers & Packer 2017](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1709h.pdf)).

### 1.2 Comparative Analysis Across Three Dimensions

*Methodological Note:* Synthesizing [ICMA (2020](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Regulatory/Green-Bonds/June-2020/External-Review-GuidelinesJune-2020-090620.pdf), [2021](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/Guidelines-for-GreenSocialSustainability-and-Sustainability-Linked-Bonds-External-Reviews-February-2021-170221.pdf), [2022)](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/documents/Sustainable-finance/2022-updates/External-Review-Guidelines_June-2022-280622.pdf), [Ehlers & Packer (2017)](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1709h.pdf), [Dorfleitner et al. (2021)](https://d-nb.info/1233658204/34), [EU SWD(2021) 181](https://www.eu.dk/samling/20211/kommissionsforslag/KOM(2021)0391/forslag/1799527/2428188.pdf), and Chinese rating methodologies: **Standardization Level** measures the extent to which criteria, audit steps, and report structures are fixed by uniform external rules; **Comparability** measures investors’ ability to equate or rank results across issuers and providers (*binary threshold* vs. *ordinal ranking* comparability); and **Information Quantity** measures narrative breadth and technical/impact granularity in the public output. Together they reveal a **structural trilemma**: certification maximizes standardization and binary comparability, SPOs maximize information quantity, and no single mechanism wins all three simultaneously.

| Attribute / Dimension | 1. Second-Party Opinion (SPO) | 2. Certification | 3. Verification (Assurance) | 4. Green Bond Rating / Scoring |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Core Mechanism** | Qualitative/technical assessment of framework alignment with GBP 4 pillars, project greenness, strategy, & ESG risks | Conformity test against science-based external standard & sector criteria, granting a formal binary label | Predefined audit/attestation procedures (ISAE 3000) checking UoP compliance, internal controls, & fund tracking | Quantitative/semi-quantitative scorecard assigning weighted scores to UoP, impact, governance, & disclosure |
| **Coverage Timing** | Pre-issuance (covers unspent proceeds); no ex post tracking unless separately commissioned | Pre-issuance certification + mandatory post-issuance assurance confirmation (and annual reporting under CBS v3.0) | Pre-issuance compliance check & post-issuance annual fund allocation/tracking audit | Pre-issuance rating; ongoing ex post surveillance in Moody’s GBA & domestic duration reviews (18-month cap in S&P) |
| **Secondary-Market Pricing (Dorfleitner et al., 2021)** | **+3.55 bps** overall ($p<0.01$); **+5.36 bps** for *Dark Green* vs. no SPO | Statistically insignificant marginal effect (-2.57 bps, $p>0.10$) | **+2.46 bps** ($p<0.05$) | Statistically insignificant in hybrid model (+1.79 bps, $p>0.10$; +10% level in OLS) |
| **1. Standardization Level** | **Low to Medium:**<br>Anchored in GBP pillars, but relies on bespoke, provider-specific methodologies with qualitative judgment and subjective shade definitions. | **High (Highest):**<br>Governed by uniform, published, science-based sector thresholds (CBS v3.0 $1.5^\circ\text{C}$/$2^\circ\text{C}$ criteria), accredited verifier pools, and formal scheme rules. | **Medium to High:**<br>High procedural standardization under codified attestation standards (ISAE 3000, IESBA, ISO 14065), though audit steps vary (EY’s 7 steps vs. Deloitte’s 4 steps) and scope/taxonomies vary per engagement. | **Low–Medium (across agencies) / Medium–High (within agency):**<br>Fixed quantitative weights *within* one agency (Moody’s 40/20/15/15/10 vs. S&P’s 60/25/15 vs. CBR’s 4-factor + sector cap), but proprietary and unstandardized *across* agencies. |
| **2. Comparability** | **Low (to Medium within provider):**<br>Bespoke formats, incompatible shade vocabularies (CICERO vs. Vigeo vs. Sustainalytics), and 38.0% of SPOs lacking any shade require bond-by-bond evaluation. | **High (Binary Eligibility) / Low (Degree of Ambition):**<br>Highest for binary pass/fail conformity to a shared scientific floor across borders; low for ranking relative ambition among certified bonds. | **Medium (Low–Medium on relative impact):**<br>Comparable for binary procedural compliance where criteria, period, and assurance level (limited vs. reasonable) match; no metric for relative environmental impact. | **Medium Overall (Highest on Ordinal Ranking within agency):**<br>Letter grades (GB1–GB5; G1–G4/NG) or 0–100 scores allow ordinal ranking within one agency’s universe, but weakened by top-grade (GB1/G1) clustering and cross-agency scale incompatibility. |
| **3. Information Quantity** | **High (Highest):**<br>Rich narrative breadth covering framework design, technical project merits, issuer strategy, governance pitfalls, and ecological lock-in risks. | **Low–Medium (Low in mark alone / Medium–High with full reports):**<br>Public certificate is a binary mark with a brief conformity letter, though underlying criteria and annual disclosures contain detail. | **Medium (Low–Medium in public letter):**<br>Public statements are concise 2–3 page boilerplate audit checklist letters confirming procedural compliance and fund controls, silent on forward-looking environmental merits. | **Medium (Low in score alone / Medium–High with full report):**<br>Standalone score compresses assumptions, but full rating reports provide structured factor weights, sub-scores, sector caps, and environmental metrics. |

#### Analytical Nuance and Blended Chinese Reports
Investor needs drive selection: **Certification** serves binary portfolio screening under strict climate mandates; **SPOs** (adopted by 78.4% of Dorfleitner et al.’s global sample, 50.0% of onshore Chinese volume by 2021 per [CBI 2021](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-Report-2021.pdf), and 66.4% by 2023) and **Green Bond Ratings** serve active pricing of relative ambition and transition sectors; and **Verification** provides an evidential audit trail for fund reconciliation. China’s shift from Big-Four assurance toward domestic SPOs/consultancies (2018–2022) shows onshore issuers prioritized narrative flexibility over binary cross-border comparability.

In **China**, domestic reviewers typically issue a single blended **第三方评估认证报告** (Third-Party Assessment and Certification Report) combining pre-issuance framework assessment (SPO), allocation-tracking commitments (Verification), and a G1–G4/NG grade (Rating). As Zhao and Liu observed in April 2017: *“目前国内在评估认证形式上没有严格区分，基本均以顾问咨询的角色出具第二意见”* (“Currently in China there is no strict distinction in the forms of assessment and certification; providers essentially issue second opinions in a consultant-advisory role”) ([Zhao & Liu 2017](http://www.greenfinance.org.cn/displaynews.php?id=803)). This blending obscures applicable standards of care, reduces cross-border comparability, and means a domestic “certification” title does not imply compliance with an international standard. As EY Greater China Partner Li Jing (李菁) noted regarding CGBSC’s 2022 review, *“机构类型多样、认证水平参差不齐”* (diverse institution types and uneven certification levels) remained a core pain point ([Financial Times China via cdmfund.cn, July 2023](https://www.cdmfund.cn/33410.html)).

### 2.1 Regulatory Framework

China’s bond market is split between the wholesale **Interbank Bond Market** and the **Exchange Bond Market** (Shanghai and Shenzhen Stock Exchanges). From December 2015 through 2022, it operated under a segmented “multi-headed regulation” (**多头监管**) structure ([ADBI Working Paper 1072, 2020](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/562076/adbi-wp1072.pdf)). Note that **企业债券** (Enterprise Bonds, regulated by NDRC) and **公司债券** (Corporate Bonds, regulated by CSRC/exchanges) are distinct legal product regimes.

#### B. Cross-Market Harmonization in 2021–2022 and Impact on Review Coverage
Prior to 2021–2022, divergent allocation ratios (50% NDRC and 70% CSRC vs. 100% PBOC/NAFMII) and catalogues caused **32.0% of China’s labeled green bonds in 2021** (down from 46.0% in 2020; ~25% excluded for failing the 100% UoP bar) and **USD 6.6 billion (RMB 46 billion) in 2019** to be excluded from the CBI database because >5% of proceeds funded working capital/refinancing or clean-coal projects ([CBI 2020](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-2019-Research-Report_2025-02-17-163051_qijt.pdf); [CBI 2021 Policy Report](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Finance-Policy-Analysis-Report-2021.pdf)). Two reforms narrowed this gap:
1. **Green Bond Endorsed Projects Catalogue (2021 Edition) (*Yinfa [2021] No. 96*):** Jointly issued by PBOC, NDRC, and CSRC on **2 April 2021** (effective **1 July 2021**), replacing the separate 2015 catalogues, removing “clean coal” and coal-power retrofits, and adopting “Do No Significant Harm” (DNSH) (raising China’s CBI alignment rate to 62.0% in 2021) ([PBOC–NDRC–CSRC Catalogue Notice 2021](https://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c7bbfcac42e6b4c788df67b24c3881611/content.shtml)).
2. **China Green Bond Principles (*China GBP / CGBP, CGBSC Announcement [2022] No. 1*):** Published **29 July 2022** with ICMA support, establishing four core elements ([China GBP, July 2022](https://www.nafmii.org.cn/ggtz/gg/202207/P020220801631427094313.pdf); [ICMA/NAFMII Analysis, Sept 2022](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/Analysis-of-Chinas-Green-Bond-Principles.pdf)): (i) **100% Proceeds Allocation** to the 2021 Catalogue (foreign issuers may use the EU–China Common Ground Taxonomy or EU Taxonomy; *post-window context: 98.2% of Chinese labeled bonds met the 100% bar by 2024 per [CBI 2024 Briefing](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/Climate-Bonds_China-State-of-the-market-briefing-2024_July-2025.pdf)*); (ii) **Proceeds Management** via supervisory account/ledger, restricting temporary idle investments to government/policy-bank/municipal bonds $\le$ **12 months** maturity; (iii) **Disclosure and Record-Keeping** (annual disclosure; records kept $\ge$ **2 years after maturity**); and (iv) **External Review** recommended (not mandated) pre- and post-issuance (Art. 2.4.3). By end-2022, PBOC, CSRC, NAFMII, and the exchanges implemented the China GBP (**SZSE** raised its threshold from 70% to 100% on **16 September 2022**, and **SSE** prospectuses adopted 100%, e.g., [China Power International Nov 2022 Prospectus, p. 28](https://static.sse.com.cn/bond/bridge2/disclosure/announcement/c/202211/021884_20221125_BXVO.pdf)), whereas **NDRC** had “not yet expressed its position” as of September 2022 ([ICMA/NAFMII Analysis, Sept 2022](https://www.icmagroup.org/assets/Analysis-of-Chinas-Green-Bond-Principles.pdf)).

Historical data confirm that unreviewed issuances were concentrated in regulator-exempt channels:

* In **2022**, certification rates by channel were ([CGBSC July 2023 Notice](https://www.nafmii.org.cn/ztbd/lszqbzwyh/tzgg/202307/P020230717648214158733.pdf); [cdmfund.cn](https://www.cdmfund.cn/33410.html)): **Green Financial Bonds (PBOC): 100%** (73/73 issues; RMB 3,599.97亿元; 50 issuers; avg. RMB 49.3亿元); **Green DFIs (NAFMII): 90.7%** (226 issues; RMB 2,916.11亿元; 134 issuers; avg. RMB 12.9亿元); **Green Corporate Bonds (CSRC/Exchanges): 75.1%** (114 issues; RMB 1,281.33亿元; 86 issuers; avg. RMB 11.2亿元); and **Green Enterprise Bonds (NDRC): only 2 certified issues** (RMB 14.1亿元; 1 issuer; avg. #### C. Direct Statutory Regulation of GBER Conduct (*PBOC & CSRC Announcement [2017] No. 20*)
Issued **26 October 2017** (released December 2017), the 46-article *Green Bond Assessment and Certification Behavior Guidelines (Interim)* (`《绿色债券评估认证行为指引（暂行）》`) governs GBER conduct across financial bonds, corporate bonds, DFIs, and ABS (Art. 3) ([Waizi.org.cn](https://www.waizi.org.cn/doc/28705.html); [Daxin CPA](https://m.daxincpa.com/newscenter/policies/2017/1229/686.html); [PBOC/CSRC No. 20 [2017]](https://www.chinaratings.com.cn/upload/docs/2018-01-17/doc_9251516169522.pdf)):
* **Qualifications, Staffing, and 15-Day Floor (Arts. 5–9, 13, 26):** Requires CGBSC registration; credentials in rating, certification, assurance, energy, climate, or environment; professional liability insurance; and a clean **3-year** legal/integrity record (Art. 6). Every project team must combine **accounting/auditing/finance and energy/climate/environment professionals**; observe a minimum engagement period of **$\ge$ 15 working days** from contract signing to report issuance (Art. 13); and keep signed working papers for **$\ge$ 2 years after bond maturity** (Art. 26).
* **Independence and Anti-Dumping (Arts. 12–16):** Mandates independence in substance and form (`在实质和形式上保持独立性`); bars engagements with economic interest, affiliation, self-evaluation, or external pressure (`经济利益、关联关系、自我评价、外部压力`); bars promising favorable conclusions; and prohibits pricing below business cost (`不得采取低于业务成本的价格`).
* **Scope and 12 Verification Procedures (Arts. 17–21, 23–24):** Pre-issuance review checks project compliance, screening/proceeds/disclosure systems, and reasonableness of benefit targets (Art. 19). Duration review checks actual project compliance, system execution, and benefit achievement (Art. 20). Article 21 lists **12 verification methods**: 6 document checks (interviews, internal-control observation, file inspection, ledger reconciliation, regulatory queries, external confirmation) and 6 field/technical/expert procedures (on-site physical inspection, mathematical/engineering recalculation, experimental testing, external expert utilization, external institutional consultation, and others). Cross-referencing existing work is encouraged (Art. 23), while GBERs remain legally liable when citing external experts (Art. 24).
* **Four Conclusion Tiers, Duration Windows, and Permanent Revocation (Arts. 29, 31–32, 34–38, 40–43):** Standardizes conclusions into **“Compliant” (符合)**, **“No Non-Compliance Found” (未发现不符)**, **“Non-Compliant” (不符合)**, and **“Unable to Express a Conclusion” (无法发表结论的免责声明)**, while encouraging greenness-degree disclosure (Art. 29). Duration reviews are due **within 4 months of each full-year anniversary** for financial bonds (Art. 31), and **within 4 months after Year 1** plus **within 4 months of any project update** for non-financial bonds (Art. 32). A “Non-Compliant” duration finding requires an explanation/rectification plan within **10 working days** (Art. 34), rectification within **$\le$ 3 months** (Art. 35), and a clean re-audit within **2 months** thereafter—otherwise the green label is permanently revoked (**撤销绿色债券标识**, Art. 36; [Reuters, 26 Dec 2017](https://www.reuters.com/article/markets/asia/-idUSL4S1OR23Y/)). An “Unable to Express a Conclusion” report gives **2 months** to obtain a positive conclusion from a new GBER (Art. 37). Revoked labels **cannot be re-applied for**, triggering any prospectus investor put-back option (`回售选择权`, Art. 38). Articles 40–43 set annual self-inspections, spot checks, and peer cross-checks.

### 2.3 Market Competition Landscape (2018 and 2019, with Trajectory to End-2022)

Analyzing 2018–2019 competition requires separating **Caliber A (CBI/CCDC Amount Basis: onshore + offshore reviewed issuance value, all review types)** from **Caliber B (Lianhe Ratings / CGBSC Count Basis: number of certified domestic onshore issues)**. Because bank financial bonds (where Big Four auditors concentrated) are far larger than corporate/DFI bonds (where domestic consultancies dominated), the two calibers invert the 2019 leadership ranking: **EY led by bond value (28.0%)** while **Lianhe Equator overtook EY to lead domestically by deal count**.

*Key Structural Developments (2020–2022):*
1. **Corporate Restructuring Behind Category Shifts:** Starting in 2020, CRAs (such as CCXI and Pengyuan) transferred their green verification teams into specialized group green consulting subsidiaries (CCX Green Finance, Shenzhen Pengyuan Green Finance), which Lianhe classifies under “Professional/Environmental Consultancies” rather than “CRAs” ([Lianhe 2021, Fig. 2.3 Fn. 5](https://www.lhratings.com/file/57bad96a-d56c-4f3d-ada5-bbbaf224178c.pdf)). Meanwhile, accounting firms’ onshore count share fell from 28.42% (2018) to 2.76% (2021, EY only) and 3.82% (2022), as assurance became mainly an offshore product.
2. **2021 Deal-Count Duopoly and CBI Certified Breakdown:** Across 362 certified domestic issues in 2021, **Lianhe Equator (47.24%, 171)** and **CCXGF (28.18%, 102)** reached **CR2 = 75.41%** (**CR3 = 80.66%** with iGreenBank at 5.25% [19]; HHI > 3,000) ([Lianhe 2021](https://www.lhratings.com/file/f0b344e8166.pdf)). ## 3. Market Challenges and Risks

### 3.1 Market Oligopoly Risk: A Two-Tier Structure (An 80.7% Duopoly Core Above a Discount Tail)

#### The Empirical Reality: Top-Tier Concentration Coexisting with a Fragmented Discount Tail
Globally, six external review providers accounted for over three-quarters of the market ([EU SWD(2021) 181](https://www.eu.dk/samling/20211/kommissionsforslag/KOM(2021)0391/forslag/1799527/2428188.pdf)). In China, data through end-2022 reveal a two-tier pyramid:
* **Early Accounting Volume Dominance (2016–2019) to Domestic Count Duopoly (2021–2022):** Through 2019, the Big Four verified **39.5% of onshore issuers** and **59% of verified bond volume (USD 53B)** ([CPI June 2020](https://climatepolicyinitiative.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/MRV-System-Design-Recommendations-for-Green-Bonds-in-China.pdf)), with EY and Deloitte holding **CR2 = 50.0%** of reviewed volume in 2018 and the top four holding **CR4 = 64.0%** in 2019. On the domestic issue-count perimeter, rating-group subsidiaries **Lianhe Equator** and **CCX Green Finance** certified **273 of 362 deals (CR2 = 75.41%) in 2021** and **338 of 419 deals (CR2 = 80.67%) in 2022** (leaving 81 deals [19.33%] for all other 16+ providers) ([Lianhe 2021](https://www.lhratings.com/file/f0b344e8166.pdf); [Lianhe 2022 via Sina](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2023-03-22/doc-imymtfcz9616320.shtml)). CGBSC’s 2022 notice ($N=416$) confirmed **CR3 = 79% by count** ($\ge$82 issues each for Lianhe Equator, CCXGF, and Far East, dropping to 16 at #4), **CR4 = 86% by value** (+EY, each $\ge$RMB 1,200亿元, dropping below RMB 500亿元 at #5), and **CR5 = 90% in duration tracking** ([CGBSC July 2023 Notice](https://www.nafmii.org.cn/ztbd/lszqbzwyh/tzgg/202307/P020230717648214158733.pdf)). *(In offshore issuances, leadership flips back to EY China, CICERO, Sustainalytics, and HKQAA).*
* **The Fragmented Discount Tail:** Simultaneously, NAFMII (Sept 2021) and EY’s sustainability lead (2022) noted that participating institutions were too dispersed to form scale effects (*“参与机构过于分散，尚未形成规模效应”*) ([NAFMII Sept 2021](https://www.nafmii.org.cn/xhdt/202109/t20210924_197385.html); [cdmfund.cn](https://www.cdmfund.cn/33410.html)). Beneath the 2–4 dominant firms executing 79%–86% of deals sat ~14–16 smaller agencies (four of which—CTC, CQC, Anrong, Dagong Low Carbon—booked zero completed domestic issuances in 2022). In this discount tail, as the *21st Century Business Herald* reported, some entrants skipped on-site project inspections and sharply undercut fees to win business.

#### Causes of Market Oligopoly Risk
1. **Channel Transmission from the Oligopolistic Credit Rating Industry:** CCXI and Lianhe Credit Rating dominate China’s domestic bond rating market. When they transferred green teams into CCXGF and Lianhe Equator (from 2020), the subsidiaries inherited nationwide branch networks and direct relationships with underwriters and SOE/LGFV finance departments, creating near-zero contracting friction for issuers already buying credit ratings from the parent group.
2. **Reputation-Seeking Demand and Pricing Amplification:** Issuers buy top-tier brands because market pricing rewards verifier reputation: in China’s onshore market (2016–2018), verified bonds priced ~**40–60 bps tighter**, and verification from high-market-share, top-3, top-5, or international verifiers significantly *amplified* the greenium (verifier-share coefficients of $-2.27$ and $-1.68$; top-3 verifier $\times$ greenness interaction of $-0.71$) ([Deng, Tang & Zhang 2020](https://www.lse.ac.uk/economics/Assets/Documents/job-market-2025-26/JM-Zhiyao-Deng-Greenness.pdf)).
3. **Fee Cross-Subsidization, Cost-Scale Dynamics, and Discount Undercutting:** Despite Article 16 of *Announcement [2017] No. 20* barring below-cost pricing, multi-business groups (CRA conglomerates and Big Four firms) share overhead and client channels with higher-margin credit rating or audit engagements, while domestic CRA consultancies process standardized domestic reports at much lower marginal cost than Big Four assurance. Meanwhile, discount entrants undercut fees by omitting site visits, squeezing independent consultancies (SynTao, Zhongcai, iGreenBank) from both above and below. *(Post-window context: In March 2026, CGBSC revised its evaluation rules specifically to curb “low-level competition and pure price wars,” adding a 3-year evaluation cycle and suspension for failing incumbents — [People’s Daily, March 2026](https://paper.people.com.cn/zgnyb/pc/content/202603/30/content_30148753.html)).*
4. **Regulatory Entry Barriers, Track-Record Weighting, and Expertise Scarcity:** Multidisciplinary talent is scarce, and qualification rules mainly constrain *new entrants* while grandfathering incumbents (J.D. Lin, 2024). Under CGBSC’s 2021 rubric (*Operation Rules [2021] No. 1*, Annex), **20% of an agency’s score is weighted directly on prior business performance, scale, and client coverage** (historical green bond volume [`绿债评估`], carbon verification [`碳核查`], and staffing [`人员配置`]) ([CGBSC Rules 2021](https://www.nafmii.org.cn/ztbd/lszqbzwyh/tzgg/202208/P020220823663445509930.pdf); [Cailianshe Sept 2022](https://www.cls.cn/detail/1138039)). This ranked Lianhe Equator #1, CCXGF #2, and EY #3, and NAFMII’s *Notice No. 172 [2022]* restricted independent interbank practice to the top 11 firms—forcing ranks #12–#18 (including pioneers Zhongcai [#13], CQC [#14], Morningstar/Sustainalytics [#15], and SynTao [#17]) to hire two external experts per deal, echoing [EU SWD(2021) 181](https://www.eu.dk/samling/20211/kommissionsforslag/KOM(2021)0391/forslag/1799527/2428188.pdf) warnings that stringent registration favors larger entities. Across all 18 registered agencies in 2022, there were **565 audit, accounting, and environmental professionals**, with **87 new hires concentrated at larger agencies**; although Tier 2 agencies drafted/revised **77% of the 30 new internal rules** in 2022, four booked zero issued deals ([CGBSC 2022 via cdmfund.cn](https://www.cdmfund.cn/33410.html)).
5. **Large-Client Amplification:** Single mega-bank mandates (e.g., Industrial Bank’s RMB 60B with Deloitte in 2018, ICBC’s USD 5.3B with CICERO in 2019, CDB’s USD 11.56B with Lianhe Equator in 2021) concentrate value share.

#### Potential Negative Impacts of Market Oligopoly
* **Standardized Desk-Based Checklist Reviews vs. Field Scrutiny:** Despite Article 13’s 15-working-day floor, when two firms process 338 deals in a year (**1.35 bond issuances per working day** combined), due diligence risks becoming desk-based Catalogue checklist compliance rather than on-site inspection (`实地勘察`) or engineering recalculation (`验算项目环境效益`) under Article 21, while discount-tier price wars lead smaller providers to skip site visits outright.
* **Reduced Contestability and Squeeze on Independent GBERs:** Tier 2 consultancies face higher variable costs (paying external experts while bearing full legal liability under Art. 24) and lower volume, limiting investment in modeling talent, while issuers face a narrow choice set of 2–4 commercially accepted tier-one providers.
* **Systemic Methodological Propagation and Reputational Contagion (“Single Point of Failure”):** Any lenient eligibility interpretation or flawed impact formula at Lianhe Equator or CCXGF propagates across ~80% of China’s certified green bond stock, and a governance scandal at either firm would impair investor confidence across hundreds of outstanding bonds simultaneously.

### 3.2 Conflicts of Interest: Five Structural Channels and Negative Impacts

Although Article 12 of *PBOC & CSRC Announcement [2017] No. 20* mandates independence in substance and form (`在实质和形式上保持独立性`) and bars engagements involving economic interest, affiliation, self-evaluation, or external pressure, Article 16 bars promised conclusions, CGBSC scores “business isolation” (`业务隔离`), and CBI/ICMA rules require fixed fees and “Chinese Walls,” structural conflicts operated through five channels through end-2022:

1. **Channel 1 — The Voluntary “Issuer-Pays” Revenue Model (Principal–Agent Conflict):** With the partial exception of China Bond Rating (an investor-pays CRA, though its green verifications are still commissioned per issue), issuers directly select and pay the GBER (Art. 15). Because external review is voluntary across most Chinese bond regimes, reviewer revenue depends on satisfying paying issuers ([Coley 2022](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4261443); [Feng & Li 2022](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4118772)). As Banahan (2019) argues, reputational concerns are an “**insufficient counterweight** to the conflicts of interest represented by the issuer pays model,” as competition tips certifiers toward accommodating issuers ([Harvard Law School Forum, Aug 2019](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2019/08/06/the-bond-villains-of-green-investment/)). Moreover, CGBSC’s 2021 evaluation rubric assigns **6% of a reviewer’s score to issuer feedback—including 1 percentage point for the issuer’s “willingness to rehire”** (alongside 6% investor and 6% expert feedback) ([CGBSC Rules 2021, Annex](https://www.nafmii.org.cn/ztbd/lszqbzwyh/tzgg/202208/P020220823663445509930.pdf)), creating tension if issuers prefer lenient scrutiny.
2. **Channel 2 — Dual Roles Within Credit Rating Groups (Credit Rating + Green Verification Bundling):** **50.0% of CGBSC’s 18 firms** (9/18, and **63.6% [7/11] of Tier 1 firms**)—and **>80% of domestic deals**—belong to credit rating groups ([CPI June 2020](https://climatepolicyinitiative.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/MRV-System-Design-Recommendations-for-Green-Bonds-in-China.pdf); [Lin 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8860139/)). For instance, filings disclose subsidiary Dagong Low-Carbon performing the green assessment while parent Dagong Global rates the same issuer’s credit. Because multi-year credit ratings generate far higher recurring fees than one-off green verifications, green subsidiaries face commercial pressure not to issue negative green findings—especially “Non-Compliant” duration reports that would trigger mandatory rectification, permanent label revocation, and investor put-back rights under Arts. 34–38—for fear of jeopardizing the parent’s credit rating client.
3. **Channel 3 — Consulting–Certification Integration and Fund-Evaluation Bundling (“Self-Evaluation” / 自我评价 Threat):** As [Fangda Partners (August 2022)](https://www.fangdalaw.com/content/details143_8811.html) documents, two cross-business conflicts recur: (i) *Consulting-plus-Verification*, where an institution (or affiliate) provides paid ESG consulting to help an issuer select indicators or draft its Green Bond Framework and then acts as the “independent” GBER certifying that framework (a self-review threat flagged by [IOSCO FR09/21, Nov 2021](https://www.iosco.org/library/pubdocs/pdf/IOSCOPD690.pdf) and [EU SWD(2021) 181](https://www.eu.dk/samling/20211/kommissionsforslag/KOM(2021)0391/forslag/1799527/2428188.pdf)); and (ii) *Fund-Evaluation-plus-Bond-Verification*, where the same institution evaluates investment funds that invest in the green bonds it certified.
4. **Channel 4 — Financial Audit Overlap:** Big Four accounting firms providing green bond assurance may simultaneously serve as (or pitch to become) the issuer’s statutory financial auditor.
5. **Channel 5 — Opinion Shopping and Selective Publication:** Given non-standardized SPO/rating methodologies, an issuer facing a threatened “Non-Compliant” opinion or low grade (G3/G4/NG) can terminate the engagement pre-signing, narrow the scope, withhold publication, or switch reviewers. Leading international SPO providers contacted in the 2019 Harvard study reported they had **never issued a negative final recommendation** ([Harvard Law School Forum, Aug 2019](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2019/08/06/the-bond-villains-of-green-investment/)).

#### Potential Negative Impacts and Documented Market Symptoms
* **Grade Inflation and Absence of Adverse Conclusions:** Public “Non-Compliant” conclusions and low grades (G3/G4/NG) are virtually non-existent in pre-issuance disclosures, mirroring international **100% GB1 clustering** ([Ehlers & Packer 2017](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt1709h.pdf); [Dorfleitner et al. 2021](https://d-nb.info/1233658204/34)).
* **“Bad Money Drives Out Good” (劣币驱逐良币), Greenwashing, and Adverse Selection:** Opportunistic issuers obtaining third-party endorsement for bonds allocating 30%–50% of proceeds to working capital (pre-2022 unification) capture lower financing costs without genuine green investment ([Zhihu Aug 2022](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/559612820); [Chinakec 2022](https://www.chinakec.com/prod_view-1092.html)). Empirically, [Deng, Tang, and Zhang (2020)](https://www.lse.ac.uk/economics/Assets/Documents/job-market-2025-26/JM-Zhiyao-Deng-Greenness.pdf) find a **0.78** correlation between verification and proceeds greenness and show investors pay a greenium only for 100% green use of proceeds, partially penalizing greenwashing; however, they warn that “on demand” issuer-pays certification makes green investment inefficient and note their data cannot separate true project greenness from mere possession of a certificate. Conversely, a 2022 *Journal of Economics* study found uncertified Chinese green bonds carried *lower* yield spreads than certified ones, suggesting adverse selection where weaker issuers buy certification while skeptical investors discount review quality.
* **Breakdown of Duration Supervision, Empty Enforcement Record, and Spot-Check Flaws:** Because duration reviews are non-mandatory and CRA parents collect annual rating fees, reviewers lack incentives to issue “Non-Compliant” duration reports. Crucially, **no publicly reported case of a green-label revocation or formal sanction against a Chinese GBER for a conflicted opinion was recorded through end-2022**. However, CGBSC’s 2022 spot-check notice officially confirmed industry flaws in assessment methods, policy comprehension, project attribute judgment, and **fee-collection forms** (*“在评估方法制定、绿评项目实际操作中，政策文件理解、绿评项目属性、收费形式等方面存在一些瑕疵，个别评估认证项目绿评不够严谨、业务流程有待完善”*), ordering rectifications ([CGBSC 2022 via cdmfund.cn](https://www.cdmfund.cn/33410.html)).
* **Deadweight Verification Costs:** Lack of credible reviewer independence forces investors to conduct redundant due diligence or buy multiple ratings ([EU SWD(2021) 181](https://www.eu.dk/samling/20211/kommissionsforslag/KOM(2021)0391/forslag/1799527/2428188.pdf)).

### 3.3 Four Additional Institutional and Operational Bottlenecks (Through End-2022)

1. **Regulatory Fragmentation and Methodological Divergence:** Beyond four regulators and NDRC’s surviving 50% rule, *Announcement [2017] No. 20* did not mandate which of the 12 verification procedures in Art. 21 (6 desk vs. 6 field/technical) must be used or fix a national grading formula. Consequently, EY used a **7-step ISAE 3000 workflow** vs. Deloitte’s **4-step workflow**, while CBR/Lianhe Equator (G1–G4/NG), Brilliance (GB-1–GB-4/NG), CCX (G-1–G-5), Moody’s/Trucost (GB1–GB5), and S&P (0–100) used incompatible scales ([Zhihu Aug 2022, Table 2](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/559612820)), compounded by blended domestic report formats.
2. **“Heavy on Pre-Issuance Review, Light on Post-Issuance Tracking” (重发行前认证、轻存续期跟踪):** Because duration tracking remained “encouraged” across all regulators and the July 2022 *China GBP* (Art. 2.4.3), issuers avoided post-issuance audit fees: (i) **CPI and CUFE IIGF (June 2020)** found pre-issuance review covered ~73% of onshore value vs. **59%** post-issuance ([CPI 2020 MRV Report](https://climatepolicyinitiative.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/MRV-System-Design-Recommendations-for-Green-Bonds-in-China.pdf)), and among **157 of 311 domestic bonds** (2016–April 2019) with impact data, external verification documents were publicly accessible for only ~half ([CPI/IIGF June 2020, p. 17](https://www.climatepolicyinitiative.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/The-State-and-Effectiveness-of-the-Green-Bond-Market-in-China.pdf)); (ii) **CBI and SynTao (2022)** (examining **627 bonds from 382 issuers**) found post-issuance proceeds reporting covered **74% of volume**, combined proceeds-plus-impact reporting covered **31%**, and **38% of bond volume had no post-issuance external review** ([CBI/SynTao 2022, pp. 4, 10, 17](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/post-issuance_reporting_in_chinas_green_bond_market_en.pdf)).
3. **Non-Mandatory External Review Rules and a 20%–35% Unreviewed Gap:** Unlike the EU Green Bond Standard (proposed July 2021, enacted Nov 2023)—which **mandates** pre- and post-issuance verification and direct ESMA supervision ([ESMA EuGBS](https://www.esma.europa.eu/esmas-activities/investors-and-issuers/external-reviewers-european-green-bonds); [Treglia](https://www.europeanpapers.eu/e-journal/enhancing-green-bond-regulation-strengthening-oversight-market-growth-climate-justice))—China’s voluntary rules left **14.0% (2018)**, **28.0% (2019)**, and **22.0% (2021)** of volume (CBI basis) and **26.36% (2018)**, **34.52% (H1 2019)**, **25.36% (2021)**, and **19.11%–20.0% (2022)** of domestic issues unreviewed.
4. **Insufficient Disclosure Granularity, Double-Counting, Confidentiality, and Weak Civil Liability:** As of 28 February 2022, **USD 6.0 billion (RMB 38.6 billion)** of 2021 Chinese green bonds sat on CBI’s “Pending List” because neither prospectuses nor GBER reports disclosed required project-level technical metrics (e.g., 20%–30% building retrofit energy-savings targets, hydropower/geothermal lifecycle $\text{gCO}_2\text{e/kWh}$ thresholds, or sustainable agriculture methods) ([CBI 2021 Market Report](https://www.climatebonds.net/files/documents/publications/China-Green-Bond-Market-Report-2021.pdf)). Co-financed infrastructure projects risk double-counting 100% of avoided emissions unless proportional scaling (mandated by NAFMII for Carbon-Neutral DFIs) is audited ([NAFMII Special Product Guidelines 2022](https://www.nafmii.org.cn/xhdt/202203/P020220621527670901499.pdf)), while undefined disclosure boundaries risk leaking confidential issuer data ([Zhihu Aug 2022](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/559612820)). Finally, supervision relied on self-regulatory sanctions (Arts. 40–43; [SZGFA](https://www.szgfa.com/news_view.aspx?id=1379&nid=2&typeid=4)) without a statutory civil liability regime compensating investors for negligent verification, and label revocation (Art. 38) does not accelerate bond principal unless prospectus put options (`回售选择权`) are included.
